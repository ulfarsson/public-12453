# Adversarial audit of the Lean certificate — 2026-09-05

**Target.** `Av12453.TwoThreshold.av12453_count_kernel : ∀ n, G (n, 0) = (avoiders n {beta 2}).card`
in `formal/Av12453`, claimed to certify the counting algorithm of
`paper/av12453_polytime.tex` §§2–6 (`eq:K`, `eq:D`, `eq:G`, `eq:answer-G`).

**Method.** Synthesis of four independent lens audits (trusted base, faithfulness-to-paper,
soundness hygiene, structure), with every finding re-verified from the Lean source, the
`.tex`, and fresh Lean/Python runs. The prior `REPORT_*.md` files were not trusted. Nothing
in the repository was modified (`git status --porcelain` empty).

**Verdict: solid, with scope caveats.** No BLOCKER, no MAJOR. The theorem is unconditional,
axiom-clean, non-circular, and means what it says. Every confirmed finding is about *claim
calibration and process durability*, not about the mathematics or the proof.

## 1. Confirmed findings

### MINOR 1 — `formal/README.md` overstates what is certified: the quotient algorithm and the published data are outside it

> "The `d = 2` kernel theorem is the correctness statement of the paper's polynomial-time
> algorithm for `12453`."

What is certified is the **unreduced** `d = 2` kernel, `eq:K`/`eq:D`/`eq:G`/`eq:answer-G`.
The paper itself calls that specialization `O(N^8)` time and `O(N^5)` storage
(`av12453_polytime.tex:222-226`); the advertised `O(N^7)`/`O(N^4)` for `Av(12453)` comes
from the two translation quotients of `sec:d2-support` — `lem:d2-exact-support` (tex:1681),
`lem:d2-translation` (tex:1706), `eq:R-recurrence` (tex:1766),
`lem:d2-second-translation` (tex:1792), `cor:d2-complexity` (tex:1874). **None of these is
in the Lean development.** I confirmed this two ways: grep for translation/quotient/`R_`
content in the twenty-six modules of the two libraries returns nothing, and a body-level
transitive-closure walk of `av12453_count_kernel` (below) touches no such constant. The
only `sec:d2-support` fragment present is `K_layer_zero`
(`Av12453/TwoThreshold/KernelFactor.lean`).

Consequence worth stating plainly: `prop:exact-150` — the shipped
`code/data/av12453_terms_0_150.txt` and `_0_300.txt` — was produced by
`av12453_fast_rns.cpp`, which "implements `\eqref{eq:R-recurrence}` and the empty-stack
recurrence `\eqref{eq:G}`" (tex:1934-1938). **The Lean theorem does not certify the code
path that generated the published coefficients.**

`formal/README.md` already discloses the gap ("what is deliberately not formalized
(complexity, `thm:protected-tail-principle`, `sec:quotients`, general `d`)") further down;
the opening paragraph contradicts it.

*Fix:* soften the opening paragraph to "the correctness statement of the unreduced `d = 2`
kernel recurrence (`eq:K`/`eq:D`/`eq:G`); the `O(N^7)` quotient of `sec:d2-support` and the
complexity bounds are not formalized."

### MINOR 2 — no complexity is formalized, and the Lean `K`/`G` are not the dynamic program

There is no cost model, memo table, or evaluation-order object anywhere in the twenty-six
modules of the two libraries. `K` and `G` are fuel-driven recursions that re-evaluate every
subcall (`Av12453/TwoThreshold/Kernel.lean`: `Kaux`, `K`, `Gaux`, `G`), not the memoized
sparse-row DP of `sec:algorithm` (tex:1594-1607).

This is not theoretical: dumping Lean's `K ℓ p t` for `ℓ ≤ 4`, `‖p‖₁ ≤ 4`, `‖t‖₁ ≤ 6`
together with `G p` for `‖p‖₁ ≤ 8` **exceeded a 600 s timeout in the Lean interpreter**,
while my Python transcription of the same recurrence with memoization returns the same
values in well under a second. The Lean object is the right *function*; it is emphatically
not the *algorithm* whose cost the paper analyses.

What *is* machine-checked from `sec:algorithm` is its mathematical content: `lem:support`
(`Av12453/TwoThreshold/KernelSupport.lean`: `K_eq_zero_of_mass_lt`, `K_support`) and the
fact that grade `w = ‖p‖₁ + ℓ` and mass `m` are well-founded orders (the termination
measures of `Kaux`/`Gaux` and of the strong induction in
`Av12453/TwoThreshold/KernelCount.lean`, `G_eq_H_aux`).

*Fix:* one sentence in `README.md`; already correct in `REPORT_component_4b.md §9.4`.

### MINOR 3 — `example` declarations are invisible to the axiom sweep, contradicting the documentation

`Av12453/Axioms.lean` (module docstring) and `formal/README.md` both claim the sweep covers
"`private` declarations, `example`s and auto-generated constants". **The `example`s are not
covered.** Verified directly in the environment: the sources contain **112** `example`
declarations, but there are **0** constants whose name mentions `_example`, and
``env.find? `_example`` is `false` — Lean elaborates `example`s under `withoutModifyingEnv`,
so they leave no constant behind.

This cannot make the headline theorem false (nothing can depend on a constant that does not
exist). But it is a real coverage gap, because this development uses `example`s for two
audit functions a referee would assume are guarded:

* the **frozen-interface statement-drift checks** — the `example`s of
  `Av12453/TwoThreshold/KernelCount.lean` that restate `G_eq_H_of`,
  `av12453_count_kernel_of`, `K_eq_zero_of_mass_lt`, `K_support`, `H_factor`, `G_eq_H` and
  `av12453_count_kernel`, and those of `Av12453/TwoThreshold/KernelFactor.lean` (its
  "Interface and sanity checks" section) that restate `H_factor`, `H_factor_zero`,
  `K_layer_zero` and `K_fst_zero`;
* roughly thirty numeric `by decide` cross-checks (e.g. the `G p = H p []` checks in the
  "Cross-checks" section of `Av12453/TwoThreshold/KernelCount.lean` and the kernel-row and
  factorization checks at the end of `Av12453/TwoThreshold/KernelFactor.lean`).

A `sorry` in any of these would emit only a build warning and pass the sweep silently.

*Fix:* correct the two doc claims, and/or convert the frozen-interface checks from
`example` to named `private theorem`s so the sweep reaches them.

### MINOR 4 — nothing automated enforces sorry-freeness

Verified: `lake env lean` on a file containing `theorem t : False := by sorry` and
`example : False := by sorry` emits two `warning: declaration uses 'sorry'` and **exits 0**.

future edit introducing a `sorry` would ship green.

*Fix:* add `lake env lean Av12453/Axioms.lean` as a CI step.

### MINOR 7 (paper prose, not Lean) — `sec:algorithm`'s kernel-phase paragraph drops the `a,b ≥ 1` side condition

`av12453_polytime.tex:1596-1600`: "In each split term, for every $a+b=\ell-1$ and
$\mathbf u\in\supp K_a(\mathbf p,\cdot)$, add …" — versus `eq:K` at tex:1502-1504, which
carries $\sum_{a,b\geq1,\;a+b=\ell-1}$.

I confirmed this is materially consequential, not pedantry. Implementing the paragraph

```

without a,b >= 1  (algo para):   1, 1, 2, 7, 38, 272, 2300, 21725, 222630
```

— wrong from `n = 3`. `eq:K` itself is unambiguous and Lean is correct
(`Finset.Ico 1 (ℓ - 1)` in `Av12453/TwoThreshold/Kernel.lean`, `K_succ_eq`). Repeating
"with $a,b\geq1$" in the kernel-phase paragraph closes it.

## 2. Notes (no action required, recorded so nothing is mistaken for a claim)

* **NOTE A — "Lean `K` = the paper's `K_ℓ(p,t)`" is a by-eye step, not a theorem.** The
  paper defines `K_ℓ^L(p,t)` *semantically* as a stopped-path weight (tex:1449-1475) and
  derives `eq:K` via `prop:kernel-recurrence` and the unformalized
  `thm:protected-tail-principle`. Lean **inverts** this: it takes `eq:K`/`eq:D`/`eq:G` as
  *definitions* and proves the consequence that matters. This is the safe direction and
  leaves no gap in the headline theorem, whose statement mentions neither `K` nor path
  semantics and whose chain is Lean-`K` → `H_factor` → `G_eq_H` → `av12453_count` →
  `avoiders`. But the identification with the paper's object rests on transcription
  fidelity, which a referee must check by eye (see §3) — I checked it line by line and
  reproduced it numerically (§4).
* **NOTE B — the truncated control sums are not merely harmless, they are necessary.**
  `eq:K` and `eq:G` sum over all controls; Lean sums over `S p = ctrls ‖p‖₁`
  (`Av12453/TwoThreshold/Kernel.lean`: `S`, and its uses inside `Kaux` and `Gaux`). With an
  unrestricted `u`, the split term's second factor `K_b(u,t)` has unbounded grade
  `‖u‖₁ + b`, so **`eq:K` read literally is not a well-founded recursion until
  `lem:support` is available**. Lean's order — truncate,
  define, prove `K_eq_zero_of_mass_lt`/`K_support` about the defined `K`, then restate the
  equations over `ctrls M` for arbitrary `M ≥ ‖p‖₁`
  (`Av12453/TwoThreshold/KernelSupport.lean`: `K_succ_eq_ctrls`, `G_eq_ctrls`;
  `Av12453/TwoThreshold/KernelFactor.lean`, `H_factor_ctrls`) — is the only sound one, and
  is not circular. I confirmed numerically that an untruncated computation over a mass-14
  box gives identical values and zero `eq:support` violations.
* **NOTE C — `D 0 p t` is a junk value.** `Av12453/TwoThreshold/Kernel.lean`, `D`; with ℕ
  subtraction, `D 0 p t = 2 * K 0 p t = 2·1_{p=t}`, outside `eq:D`'s domain
  (tex:1508-1512, `ℓ ≥ 1`). Confirmed inert: `D` is reached only through `D_one`, `D_succ`
  (`2 ≤ ℓ`) and inside `K_succ_eq` (`1 ≤ ℓ`); no application at `ℓ = 0` exists anywhere.
* **NOTE D — `PermPatterns.OrderIso` shadows Mathlib's `OrderIso`**
  (`PermPatterns/Word.lean`, `OrderIso`). Inside `namespace PermPatterns` an unqualified
  `OrderIso` is the list relation, not `≃o`. Readability hazard only; the printed
  theorem statement is fully
  qualified and all 1438 project constants are rooted at `PermPatterns` or `Av12453`.
  **Resolved on 2026-09-19** by the rename to `PermPatterns.OrderIsomorphic`: no bare
  `OrderIso` remains in either library. See §7.
* **NOTE E — `Contains w [] ` is true for every `w`, so `avoiders n {[]} = ∅`** (confirmed:
  `#eval` gives `[0,0,0,0,0,0]`). Standard convention; no bearing on `beta 2` (length 5).
* **NOTE F — six of the 24 `#eval` blocks are unguarded by `#guard_msgs`**
  (`Av12453/OneThreshold/Kernel.lean` ×2, `Av12453/OneThreshold/Counting.lean` ×1,
  `Av12453/TwoThreshold/Kernel.lean` ×1, `Av12453/TwoThreshold/KernelCount.lean` ×1,
  `Av12453/TwoThreshold/KernelFactor.lean` ×1); one prints on every build. `#eval` is
  compiler-evaluated, not kernel-checked, and no theorem depends on any of them. The 18
  guarded ones do fail the build if a value changes.
* **NOTE G — the manuscript never mentions the certificate.** `grep -i
  "lean|formaliz|machine-check|certificate"` over `av12453_polytime.tex` returns **nothing**.
  As things stand the Lean development carries no weight for the submission; if it is meant
  to support the paper it must be cited and deposited with the supplement.
* **NOTE H — correction to a prior audit report.** The soundness-hygiene audit claimed an
  "independent cache-free deep axiom walk … traversing `(env.setExporting false).checked.get`"
  via `ConstantInfo.value?`. **That method does not work on this toolchain.** I verified
  that `value?` returns `none` for imported theorems in the default, non-exporting *and*
  kernel environments, so such a walk silently visits only *types*: my faithful
  reproduction of it visited 725 constants and found **zero** axioms — not the claimed
  three. The walk must match `.thmInfo v => v.value` explicitly. Done correctly (§4), the
  conclusion stands, but it stands on the corrected method, not the reported one.

**Statement, axioms, non-circularity**

* `#check` gives exactly `∀ (n : ℕ), TwoThreshold.G (n, 0) = (PermPatterns.avoiders n {beta 2}).card`
  — no hypotheses, no free variables (`relaxedAutoImplicit = false` in `lakefile.toml`).
* `#print` shows the proof term is
  `fun n => av12453_count_kernel_of (fun _ℓ p L hℓ => H_factor hℓ p L) n` — the
  `TwoThresholdFactorization` hypothesis is discharged, not carried.
* `#print axioms` → `[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]`. Same for
  `av12453_count`, `H_factor`, `K_support`, `G_eq_H`, `av1342_count`,
  `av1342_count_kernel`, `avoids_beta_iff_forall_trigger`.

  `OrderIsomorphic`, `beta`;

* Lean's `(avoiders n {beta 2}).card` for `n ≤ 7` is `1,1,2,6,24,119,694,4581` — identical
  to my brute force, so `Contains`/`avoiders` denote the right set, not merely the right
  count. Controls: `avoiders n {beta 1}` = `1,1,2,6,23,103,512,2740` = `|Av_n(1342)|`;
  `perms n` = `n!` for `n ≤ 7`; `avoiders n {pat231}` = Catalan.
* `Contains` edge cases behave correctly: `Contains [0,1,3,4,2] (beta 2) = true`,
  `Contains [1,1,0] 231 = false` (repeats cannot realize distinct entries),
  `Contains [0,0,1] [0,0] = true`, `Contains [30,90,10] 231 = true` (scaling-invariant).
* `contains_12453_iff` (`Av12453/Basic.lean`) is a **proved** equivalence with the
  classical index definition, and its rank pattern in position order is `1,2,4,5,3 = 12453`.
* The `eq:K` transcription is faithful term by term (table in §3), checked by hand against
  tex:1115-1127 and tex:1494-1556.
* The fuel is provably adequate: `K_succ_eq` and `G_eq`
  (`Av12453/TwoThreshold/Kernel.lean`) hold **unconditionally** (only `1 ≤ ℓ`), with no
  fuel side condition, and `Kaux_congr`/`Gaux_congr` prove fuel-independence above the
  grade. I checked by hand that every
  right-hand-side occurrence has grade `< w`, so `K_zero` + `K_succ_eq` pin `K` as the
  unique solution of a well-founded recurrence.
* The chain is unconditional end to end: `av12453_count_kernel` → `G_eq_H_of` +
  `KernelFactor.H_factor` → `Counting.av12453_count` (itself unconditional, with
  `DeferredHyp`/`CompleteHyp` discharged by `deferredHyp`/`completeHyp`). No Prop-packaged
  hypothesis survives into any statement.
* Cross-check inside the development: `Av12453/TwoThreshold/Counting.lean` ends with a
  `#guard_msgs`-guarded `#eval` comparing `(avoiders k {beta 2}).card` against `H (k,0) []`
  for `k ≤ 7` — a live regression check that would fail the build if either drifted.

**Non-vacuity**

`G (n,0)` diverges from `n!` at `n = 5` (119 vs 120), so `avoiders` is a genuine proper
subset of `perms`; the split terms with `ℓ ≥ 2` are genuinely exercised in the recursion
below `(n,0)`; and `G`'s closure does not mention `avoiders`, so the equality is not
definitional.

## 5. Plain-language verdict for the author

The certificate is solid. I tried hard to break it and could not: the headline theorem is
unconditional, uses only Lean's three standard axioms (verified twice, the second time by a
walk over the actual proof bodies that ignores the axiom tables baked into the `.olean`
files), contains no `sorry` or `native_decide`, sits on an unmodified Mathlib at the pinned
`v4.33.1`, and equates two objects that are defined independently of each other — so it is
not circular and not trivially true. Every definition in its statement means what it claims:
`avoiders n {beta 2}` really is the set of `12453`-avoiding permutations (I re-enumerated
them by brute force and got the same sets and the same counts `1,1,2,6,24,119,694,4581`),
and `G` really is `eq:K`/`eq:D`/`eq:G` (I re-implemented those from the `.tex` with the
paper's *unrestricted* sums and matched every Lean value). Nothing I found threatens the
mathematics. What I would change is the surrounding prose. `formal/README.md` says the
theorem is "the correctness statement of the paper's polynomial-time algorithm for 12453";
it is the correctness statement of the *unreduced* `d = 2` recurrence, which the paper
itself calls the `O(N^8)`/`O(N^5)` version — the advertised `O(N^7)`/`O(N^4)`, and the
program that actually produced the published 150- and 300-term data, both go through the
translation quotients of §7, none of which is formalized, and no complexity claim is
formalized at all. Two smaller process items: the sweep does not in fact cover `example`
declarations (so the frozen-interface drift checks and the numeric `decide`s are unguarded),
and nothing automated runs it, since CI only does `lake build` and a `sorry` is merely a
warning there. Add the sweep to CI, fix the three overstatements (README:14, the `example`
claim, and §9.2's placement of `Trigger.lean`/`FirstLetter.lean` in the chain when they are
in fact used by nothing), ship a LICENSE file, add "with `a,b ≥ 1`" to the kernel-phase
paragraph in `sec:algorithm`, and cite the certificate somewhere in the manuscript — which
currently never mentions it at all.
