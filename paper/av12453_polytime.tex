       avoiding a direct sum of an increasing pattern and 231}
\author{Henning Ulfarsson}

\begin{abstract}
We give an exact algorithm counting the permutations that avoid a fixed
pattern from the following family: the direct sum of an increasing pattern
and the pattern 231.  The first members of the family are 1342 and 12453.
For each member, the algorithm computes the number of avoiding permutations
of every length up to a given bound using polynomially many arithmetic
operations and polynomially many stored integers, with degrees that grow
linearly in the length of the pattern.  We first obtain an exact recurrence by reading a permutation
from left to right and recording, at each step, the constraints that the
letters read so far impose on those still unread.  Its state space grows
exponentially, so evaluating it directly takes exponential time.  We then
show that part of the state is protected: later steps carry it along
unchanged and do not depend on it.  Factoring the protected part out turns
the recurrence into a dynamic program with polynomially many stored transfer
entries, and this gives the polynomial bounds for every member of the
family.  For the
pattern 12453, a translation symmetry sharpens the bounds to degree seven
for the operations and degree four for the storage.  Separately
written implementations and exact Chinese-remainder certification determine
the number of 12453-avoiding permutations of every length up to 150.  The
previously published series reached length 38.  The same tables also generate uniformly random avoiders in polynomial
time.  We illustrate this with a heatmap of one million
12453-avoiding permutations of length 300 sampled with floating-point tables.
The counting recurrences for 1342 and 12453 are verified in the Lean~4
proof assistant.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A permutation $\pi=\pi_1\cdots\pi_n$ of
$[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}$ \emph{contains} a pattern
$\tau$ in the symmetric group $S_k$ if there are indices
$1\leq i_1<\cdots<i_k\leq n$ for which
$\pi_{i_1},\ldots,\pi_{i_k}$ has the same relative order as $\tau$.
Otherwise $\pi$ \emph{avoids} $\tau$.  For example, the entries
$2,5,7,9,6$ in $2\,5\,1\,7\,9\,6\,8\,3\,4$ have relative ranks
$1,2,4,5,3$, and therefore form an occurrence of $12453$.  We write
$\Av_n(\tau)$ for the set of $\tau$-avoiding
permutations of $[n]$, and $\Av(\tau)=\bigcup_{n\geq0}\Av_n(\tau)$.
Throughout, $\N=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}$.

The basic enumerative problem is to determine
\[
  a_n(\tau)=|\Av_n(\tau)|.
\]
Here ``polynomial-time enumeration'' means computing this integer, not
listing the avoiding permutations.  This is the algorithmic notion behind
Wilfian formulas and systematic methods based on enumeration schemes and
insertion encodings
\cite{AlbertLintonRuskuc2005,Vatter2008,Zeilberger1998}.  For a fixed pattern,
the Marcus--Tardos theorem gives an exponential upper bound
$a_n(\tau)\leq c(\tau)^n$ on the counting sequence
\cite{MarcusTardos2004}.  The task is to obtain $a_n(\tau)$ in at most $P(n)$
steps, where $P$ is a polynomial.
No general principle
supplies such an algorithm:
Garrabrant and Pak constructed a finitely based class whose exact counting
sequence cannot be computed in polynomial time unless
$\mathsf{EXP}=\mathsf{\oplus EXP}$ \cite{GarrabrantPak2016}.\footnote{Here
$\mathsf{EXP}$ is the class of decision problems solvable in deterministic
exponential time, $\mathsf{\oplus EXP}$ is the class of problems that ask
whether a nondeterministic exponential-time machine has an odd number of
accepting computations, and the two classes are widely believed to differ.}

If $\alpha\in S_r$ and $\beta\in S_s$, their \emph{direct sum}
$\alpha\oplus\beta$ is the word obtained by writing $\alpha$ and then
writing $\beta$ with every value increased by $r$.  We will focus on the following direct-sum
family.  Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:family}
 \iota_d=12\cdots d,
 \qquad
 \beta_d=\iota_d\oplus231
 =12\cdots d\,(d+2)(d+3)(d+1).
\end{equation}
Thus
\[
 \beta_1=1342,\qquad \beta_2=12453,\qquad
 \beta_3=123564,\qquad \beta_4=1234675.
\]
The case $d=1$ is already enumerated algebraically by B\'ona
\cite{Bona1997}.
The family itself also appears in B\'ona's later work: from
$\beta_d=1\oplus\beta_{d-1}$ for $d\geq2$, and $\beta_1=1\oplus231$, he obtained
a recursive deletion rule for
left-to-right minima (entries smaller than every entry to their left) and
used it to determine the Stanley--Wilf limits
\cite{Bona2005}.  That rule does not by itself determine the exact counts
from the unrefined $\beta_{d-1}$ counts.
The next case is $d=2$, giving $\beta_2 = 12453$.  The counting sequence begins
\begin{equation}\label{eq:first-terms}
  1,1,2,6,24,119,694,4581,33286,260927,2174398,19053058,\ldots.
\end{equation}

In the 2022 census, the $12453$ sequence was known through $n=38$ from a
specialized program of Biers-Ariel
\cite{BiersAriel2019,BiersArielA116485,ClisbyConwayGuttmannInoue2022}.  The program uses a
composition-valued state of unbounded length, and the number of its states
grows exponentially.  Our starting point is the same recurrence: for $d=2$
the literal recurrence developed below coincides with Biers-Ariel's, state
for state, and we extend it to the whole family $\beta_d$.  We then show how
to shrink its exponential state space to polynomial size.

The family also has a description by a sorting device.  Albert, Homberger,
Pantone, Shar, and Vatter \cite{AlbertHombergerPantoneSharVatter2018} study
the $\mathcal C$-machine, which pushes each input entry anywhere into a
container whose contents must remain in the class $\mathcal C$ and pops the
container from its left end.  Their Theorem~1.1 shows that the
$\Av(B)$-machine generates exactly $\Av(1\ominus B)$, and they observe that
$\Av(4213)$, the complement of $\Av(1342)$, is generated by the
$\Av(213)$-machine, answering a question of B\'ona.  Since
$\beta_d^{\,c}=1\ominus\beta_{d-1}^{\,c}$ for the complement
$\beta_d^{\,c}$, the class $\Av(\beta_d)$ is, up to complementation, the class
generated by the $\Av(\beta_{d-1}^{\,c})$-machine.  For $12453$ the container
class is $\Av(4213)$.  They prove that the generated class has a rational
generating function when $\mathcal C$ is finite, an algebraic one when
$\mathcal C$ is bounded, and a Wilfian formula, that is, a polynomial-time
counting algorithm \cite{Zeilberger1998}, when $\mathcal C$ is a polynomial
class.  The container class $\Av(4213)$ has growth rate $8$ and is none of
these, so their sufficient conditions do not apply to $12453$.  The
protected-tail factorization below supplies the Wilfian formula outside
them.

In a permutation that avoids $\beta_d$, an increasing subsequence of length $d$ creates a condition on a future
$231$-avoiding projection, and the conjunction of the conditions created by a
prefix is represented by an ordered stack of intervals.  A transition
changes the control (the counts of unread values that no obligation created
so far restricts), the active head of the stack, or both.  The deferred
tail remains unchanged until every interval preceding it has been consumed,
and the transitions before that time do not depend on the tail.  A transfer
kernel summarizes this part of the computation by recording the control at
which the tail becomes available.  This factorization removes the stack from
the memoization key, and the resulting transfer table has polynomial size.

Our main result is the following.
The notation $O_d(\cdot)$ allows the hidden constant to depend on the fixed
family parameter $d$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every fixed $d\geq1$ and every $N\geq0$, the numbers
\[
  |\Av_0(\beta_d)|,\ldots,|\Av_N(\beta_d)|
\]
can be computed using $O_d(N^{3d+2})$ exact-integer arithmetic operations
and $O_d(N^{2d+1})$ stored integers.  All integers involved have
$O_d(N\log N)$ bits.
Consequently the bit complexity\footnote{The operation count treats each
integer addition or multiplication as one step.  The bit complexity counts
operations on single bits, so the sizes of the integers enter through the multiplication cost $M(b)$
defined below.}
is polynomial.  More precisely it is
\[
  O_d\!\left(N^{3d+2} M(N\log N)\right),
\]
where $M(b)$ is the cost of multiplying $b$-bit integers.
\end{theorem}

In particular, the family theorem gives time $O(N^5)$ and storage $O(N^3)$
for $\Av(1342)$, and $O(N^{8})$ and $O(N^5)$ for $\Av(12453)$.  An additional
translation symmetry, proved in \cref{sec:d2-support}, improves this to
$O(N^7)$ and $O(N^4)$ for $\Av(12453)$ (\cref{cor:d2-complexity}).  The
theorem is polynomial for each fixed $d$.  It is not a uniform polynomial
bound when $d$ is part of the input.

To our knowledge, no exact polynomial-time enumeration algorithm was
previously available for the family $\Av(\iota_d\oplus231)$ at every fixed
$d$, or even for the single class $\Av(12453)$.  The contributions are
threefold.  First, we show that the literal recurrence is a pushdown
system: every transition acts on the interval stack through its active head
alone, so the completion counts factor through transfer kernels
(\cref{thm:protected-tail-principle,cor:protected-tail}) despite
Fibonacci-many reachable literal stack states (\cref{prop:exponential}).
The factorization itself is the counting form of the classical conversion
of a pushdown automaton into a grammar (\cref{rem:pop-sequences}).  What is
new is that it applies with a control set that grows with $n$, where it
yields a transfer table of polynomial size rather than a finite grammar.
Second, we prove explicit family bounds
(\cref{thm:main}) and two translation quotients for $12453$
(\cref{lem:d2-translation,lem:d2-second-translation}).  Third, we provide
separately written implementations and a Chinese-remainder certification of
the values $|\Av_n(12453)|$ for $n\leq150$ (\cref{prop:exact-150}).
In addition, the transfer tables give uniform random generation of avoiders
in polynomial time (\cref{prop:sampling}), and the counting recurrences for
$d\leq2$ have been machine-checked in Lean~4 (\cref{sec:formal}).

\section{Triggers, stacks, and the complete one-threshold prototype}
\label{sec:obligations}

We first describe the restrictions that a scanned prefix imposes on its
completions and show that they admit an interval-stack representation for
every $d\geq1$.  This part
(\cref{lem:trigger,lem:first-letter,lem:legal-moves,lem:merger,prop:scan-states})
is reused unchanged for the general family in \cref{sec:literal}, and \cref{thm:protected-tail-principle} is stated for recurrences on a control
set and a stack, so that it applies verbatim in \cref{sec:kernels}.  We then develop the counting
algorithm completely for $d=1$.  This case isolates the protected-tail
construction before the additional threshold coordinates are introduced.

For a word $w$ with distinct entries, its \emph{standardization}
$\st(w)$ is obtained by replacing the smallest entry by $1$, the next
smallest by $2$, and so on.  If $X$ is a set of values, $w|_X$ denotes the
subword formed by retaining only the entries belonging to $X$.  An
\emph{increasing $d$-subsequence} of a permutation $\pi$, or more generally of a
word $\pi$ with distinct entries, is a choice of positions
$i_1<\cdots<i_d$ with
$\pi_{i_1}<\cdots<\pi_{i_d}$.  We call its last entry a
\emph{$d$-trigger}.  The positions need not be adjacent.

\begin{lemma}[Trigger lemma]\label{lem:trigger}
A permutation $\pi=\pi_1\cdots\pi_n$ avoids $\beta_d$ if and only if, for
every increasing $d$-subsequence ending at $c=\pi_j$, the word
\[
  \pi_{j+1}\cdots\pi_n\big|_{\{x:x>\pi_j\}}
\]
avoids $231$ after standardization.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The first $d$ entries of an occurrence of $\beta_d$ form an increasing
$d$-subsequence, and its last three entries form a $231$ whose values are all
larger.  Conversely, an increasing $d$-subsequence followed by such a $231$
has relative order $\iota_d\oplus231=\beta_d$.
\end{proof}

When a trigger $c$ is read, its entire subsequent projection onto values
larger than $c$ must avoid
$231$.  Later in the scan, some letters of that projection have already been
read.  Merely testing the still-unread projection would lose mixed
occurrences involving both the read and unread portions.  The interval stack
below records the resulting conditions on the unread letters.

The \emph{$231$-obligation created by a trigger $c=\pi_j$} is the requirement
that
\(
 \pi_{j+1}\cdots\pi_n|_{\{x:x>c\}}
\)
avoid $231$ after standardization.  For a trigger $c$ in a scanned prefix,
its \emph{residual obligation} is the restriction of this requirement to
completions of that prefix, with the already scanned letters of the
projection fixed: if $\sigma=\sigma_1\cdots\sigma_k$ is the prefix,
$c=\sigma_j$, the letters of $[n]$ not in $\sigma$ are \emph{unread}, and $w$ is a
\emph{completion} of $\sigma$, that is, a word using each unread letter once,
then $w$ satisfies the residual obligation of
$c$ when $(\sigma_{j+1}\cdots\sigma_k w)|_{\{x:x>c\}}$ avoids $231$ after
standardization.

\subsection{The Catalan interval stack}\label{sec:stack}

The following first-letter decomposition of $\Av(231)$ applies to an
arbitrary finite ordered alphabet.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}[First-letter lemma]\label{lem:first-letter}
Let $I$ be a finite totally ordered set of size $\ell$, and let $x\in I$ have
local rank $r=|\{y\in I:y\leq x\}|$.  A permutation of $I$ beginning with
$x$ avoids $231$ if and only if
all values below $x$ occur before all values above $x$, and the two induced
subwords avoid $231$.  Thus reading $x$ replaces an interval of size $\ell$
by intervals of sizes
\[
  r-1,\qquad \ell-r,
\]
in that order, with zero parts deleted.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If an upper value occurs before a lower value after $x$, these three entries
have pattern $231$.  Conversely, if the lower and upper subwords occur in
that order, a $231$ cannot use letters from both: both position and value
increase from the first block to the second.  Nor can a $231$ use $x$ and
two letters from just one block: $x$ is larger than every lower letter and
smaller than every upper letter, whereas the first entry of a $231$ has
middle rank.  If it uses $x$, one lower letter, and one upper letter, their
order is $213$.  Avoidance therefore reduces to avoidance inside the two
blocks.
\end{proof}

Write $\Av(231)(I)$ for the $231$-avoiding words using each value of $I$
once.  If $I<J$, that is, every element of $I$ is smaller than every
element of $J$, and $u$ and $w$ are words on $I$ and $J$, then $u\oplus w$
is the concatenation $uw$.  For sets of such words, $\oplus$ denotes the set
of all concatenations.  Consider the projection created by one trigger $c$,
and read its letters one at a time.  By \cref{lem:first-letter}, the first
letter $x$ splits the rest of the projection into a lower and an upper
interval, and every letter of the lower interval must be read before any
letter of the upper one.  Hence the next letter of the projection lies in
the lower interval while that interval is nonempty, and the lemma, applied
to the subword on that interval, splits it in turn.  The letter itself then
plays no further role, since both conditions of the lemma concern only the
letters after it.  Repeated application
of \cref{lem:first-letter} thus represents the residual obligation of $c$
by an ordered interval stack
\[
  I_1<I_2<\cdots<I_s,
\]
that is, the unread letters of the projection must form a word in
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stack-form}
  \Av(231)(I_1)\oplus\Av(231)(I_2)\oplus\cdots
  \oplus\Av(231)(I_s).
\end{equation}
The read letters of the projection do not appear in \eqref{eq:stack-form},
and none of them is needed again: an occurrence of $231$ involving a read
letter $x$ and an unread letter must begin with $x$, because if a letter
$a<x$ read earlier were its first entry, every value of the projection below
$a$ would already have been read, since the lower interval of $a$ precedes
its upper one, which contains $x$.  Beginning with $x$, the occurrence
needs an upper letter followed by a lower one, which the split at $x$
excludes.  For instance, relabel the
values of a projection as $1,\ldots,6$ and suppose that its first two
letters are $4$ and $2$.  Reading $4$ leaves the stack
$\{1,2,3\}\mid\{5,6\}$, and reading $2$ then leaves
$\{1\}\mid\{3\}\mid\{5,6\}$.  The completion $1,3,6,5$ is allowed, whereas
a completion beginning $3,1$ is excluded: together with the read letter $2$
it would form the occurrence $2,3,1$ of $231$.

The stack records the obligations created by the scanned prefix.  Further
obligations arise as the completion is read, and they are incorporated by
the stack updates described below.  Here and throughout, an \emph{interval}
is a consecutive block of the ordered set of currently unread values.  Its
elements need not be consecutive integers.
We call $I_1$ the \emph{active head} and $(I_2,\ldots,I_s)$ the
\emph{deferred tail}.  Only the active head may be read next among the values
of the stack: every letter of $I_1$ must be read before any letter of
$I_2$, and so forth.  Unread values below the trigger are constrained by no
obligation created so far and may be interspersed.  For an interval of size $\ell$, an
\emph{endpoint choice} has local rank $1$ or $\ell$, and an \emph{interior
choice} has local rank in $\{2,\ldots,\ell-1\}$.

We call the gap in value
between two consecutive intervals of the current stack a \emph{separation},
and a read letter lying in that gap a \emph{separator}.  A separation may
have several separators.  An interior choice creates a new separation, at
the letter read, while an endpoint choice leaves at least one side empty
and creates none, although the letter read may lie in an existing
separation.  Inductively, as long as no trigger smaller than $c$ has been
read (a trigger larger than $c$ is itself a letter of the projection and
only splits it further), two unread letters $u<v$ of the projection lie in
different intervals if and only if some letter $y$ with $u<y<v$ has been
read from the projection.  \Cref{lem:1342-separators,cor:separators} below determine the separations
of the stack once the obligations of several triggers have been combined.

\begin{example}[Reading one projection]\label{ex:one-projection}
The permutation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:running-example}
 \pi=9,11,10,14,5,12,6,2,3,8,4,7,1,13,15
\end{equation}
of $[15]$ avoids $1342$, hence also $12453$, and serves as the running
example of this article.  For $d=1$ every letter is a trigger.  The
projection created by the first letter $9$ is the word
$11,10,14,12,13,15$ formed by the later letters above $9$.  It
standardizes to $215346$ and avoids $231$, as \cref{lem:trigger} requires.
We read this projection letter by letter.  The stack starts as the single
interval $\{10,\ldots,15\}$.  The first letter $11$ has local rank $2$, an
interior choice, so \cref{lem:first-letter} requires the lower interval
$\{10\}$ to be read before the upper interval $\{12,13,14,15\}$.  The stack
is $\{10\}\mid\{12,\ldots,15\}$.  Reading $10$ is an endpoint choice that
exhausts the head and leaves $\{12,\ldots,15\}$, and the interior choice
$14$, of local rank $3$ in that interval, splits it into
$\{12,13\}\mid\{15\}$.  The unread letters of the projection must now form
a word in $\Av(231)(\{12,13\})\oplus\Av(231)(\{15\})$: either order of $12$
and $13$, then $15$.  The read letters $11$, $10$, $14$ are not retained.
While $10$
was unread, $11$ was the only separator of the separation between $\{10\}$
and $\{12,\ldots,15\}$.  Now $14$ is the only separator of the separation
between $\{12,13\}$ and $\{15\}$, and $10$ and $11$ separate nothing.
\end{example}

Several triggers impose overlapping obligations.  The following definition
fixes the object that records all of them: a read prefix together with a
stack, updated by three kinds of move.  \Cref{lem:merger} and
\cref{prop:scan-states} then show that the stack of a legal prefix
represents exactly the conjunction of the residual obligations of its
triggers, and that the prefixes that can be read are exactly the prefixes
of $\beta_d$-avoiding permutations.

\begin{definition}[Scan states and legal moves]\label{def:scan-state}
Fix $d\geq1$ and $n$.  A \emph{scan state} is a pair $(\sigma,S)$, where
$\sigma$, the \emph{read prefix}, is a word with distinct letters from $[n]$,
and $S=(I_1,\ldots,I_s)$, the \emph{stack}, is a list of nonempty sets with
$I_1<I_2<\cdots<I_s$ whose union is the set of unread letters larger than
the least $d$-trigger $q$ of $\sigma$, and is empty when $\sigma$ has no
$d$-trigger.  The \emph{initial state} is $(\emptyword,\varnothing)$: the
empty prefix and the empty stack, $s=0$.  Let $x$ be an unread letter.  Reading $x$ is a \emph{legal
move}, leading to the state $(\sigma x,S')$, in exactly the following three
cases.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] \emph{Merger}: $x$ is a $d$-trigger of $\sigma x$ and is smaller
  than every $d$-trigger of $\sigma$.  Let $E$ be the set of unread letters
  strictly between $x$ and $q$, or the set of all unread letters larger than
  $x$ when $\sigma$ has no $d$-trigger.  Then $S'=(E\cup I_1,I_2,\ldots,I_s)$
  if $s\geq1$, and $S'=(E)$ if $s=0$, the empty stack when $E=\varnothing$.
\item[(b)] \emph{Split}: $x$ is a $d$-trigger of $\sigma x$ that exceeds some
  $d$-trigger of $\sigma$, and $x\in I_1$.  Then
  $S'=(I_1^{<x},I_1^{>x},I_2,\ldots,I_s)$, where $I_1^{<x}$ and $I_1^{>x}$
  are the sets of elements of $I_1$ below and above $x$, empty sets being
  deleted.  The move is an endpoint or an interior choice according to the
  local rank of $x$ in $I_1$, as defined above.
\item[(c)] \emph{Non-trigger move}: $x$ is not a $d$-trigger of $\sigma x$.
  Then $S'=S$.  For $d=1$ every letter is a $1$-trigger, so this case does
  not occur.
\end{enumerate}
Reading any other unread letter is \emph{illegal}.  A word $\sigma$ is
\emph{legal} if the initial state reaches a state $(\sigma,S)$ by legal
moves.  Then $S$ is determined by $\sigma$, and we write $\mathcal S(\sigma)$
for it and call it the stack of $\sigma$.  Finally, $\mathcal R(\sigma)$
denotes the set of permutations of $[n]$ with prefix $\sigma$ all of whose
prefixes are legal, the permutations that \emph{can be read} from $\sigma$.
\end{definition}

It is useful to note that, if $\sigma$ has a $d$-trigger, then every
unread letter $x>q$ is a $d$-trigger of $\sigma x$ exceeding $q$, since
replacing the last entry of an increasing $d$-subsequence ending at $q$ by
$x$ gives one ending at $x$.  For $d=1$ a legal move is a new left-to-right
minimum, which merges, or a letter of the active head, which splits.
\Cref{fig:merger} illustrates a merger.

\begin{lemma}[Legal moves]\label{lem:legal-moves}
Let $(\sigma,S)$ be a scan state and let $x$ be an unread letter.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] If reading $x$ is a legal move, then $(\sigma x,S')$, with $S'$
  as in \cref{def:scan-state}, is a scan state.
\item[(b)] Reading $x$ is illegal if and only if $x$ lies in a deferred
  interval $I_2,\ldots,I_s$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(a) In a merger, $x$ becomes the least $d$-trigger of $\sigma x$.  If
$\sigma$ has no $d$-trigger, then $S$ is empty and $E$ is the set of all
unread letters above $x$, so $S'=(E)$ is a scan state with $\sigma x$.
Otherwise the unread letters of $\sigma x$ larger than $x$ are those of $E$
together with those larger than $q$, that is, $E\cup I_1\cup\cdots\cup I_s$,
the union of $S'$.  Every letter of $E$ is smaller than $q$ and every letter
of $S$ is larger, so $E<I_1$ and the sets of $S'$ are disjoint and
increasing.  In a
split, the least $d$-trigger is still $q$, since $x>q$, and the unread
letters above $q$ lose exactly $x$, which is what the split removes.  The two
parts of $I_1$ lie below and above $x$.  In a non-trigger move, $x<q$ if
$\sigma$ has a $d$-trigger, since an unread $x>q$ is a $d$-trigger of
$\sigma x$ as noted above, so the least $d$-trigger and the unread letters
above it are unchanged, and so is $S$.  If $\sigma$ has no $d$-trigger, then
$S$ is empty and a non-trigger move leaves it empty.

(b) Let $x$ lie in a deferred interval.  Then $x>q$, so $x$ is a
$d$-trigger of $\sigma x$ exceeding $q$, as noted above.  Hence cases (a)
and (c) of \cref{def:scan-state} do not apply, and $x\notin I_1$ excludes
case (b).  Conversely, let $x$ be illegal.  Since case (c) does not apply,
$x$ is a $d$-trigger of $\sigma x$.  Since case (a) does not apply, $x$
exceeds some $d$-trigger of $\sigma$, so $x>q$.  Hence $x$ lies in the
stack, and in a deferred interval since case (b) does not apply.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Faithful stacks]\label{def:faithful}
Let $(\sigma,S)$ be a scan state with $S=(I_1,\ldots,I_s)$.  We say that
$S$ is \emph{faithful to} $\sigma$ if a completion $w$ of $\sigma$ satisfies
the residual obligations of all $d$-triggers of $\sigma$ precisely when
\[
  w|_{I_1\cup\cdots\cup I_s}\in\Av(231)(I_1)\oplus\cdots\oplus\Av(231)(I_s),
\]
where the right-hand side is the empty word when $s=0$.
\end{definition}

A scan state fixes the shape of the stack.  Faithfulness is the semantic
property that the stack describes exactly the obligations created so far,
and a stack may or may not have it.  \Cref{lem:merger} shows that a merger
preserves faithfulness, and \cref{prop:scan-states} that the stack
$\mathcal S(\sigma)$ of a legal prefix is faithful to $\sigma$.

\begin{example}[Faithful and unfaithful stacks]\label{ex:faithful}
Let $d=1$ and let $\sigma=9,11,10,14$ be the prefix of the running example
\eqref{eq:running-example} scanned in \cref{ex:one-projection}.  Its least
trigger is $9$, and the unread letters above $9$ are $12$, $13$, $15$, so
the stacks $(\{12,13\},\{15\})$, $(\{12,13,15\})$ and
$(\{12\},\{13\},\{15\})$ all form scan states with $\sigma$.  For $d=1$
every letter of $\sigma$ is a trigger, and a completion $w$ satisfies their
residual obligations exactly when $11,10,14,w|_{\{y:y>9\}}$ and
$14,w|_{\{y:y>11\}}$ avoid $231$, the obligations of $10$ and $14$ adding
nothing further.  As computed in \cref{ex:one-projection}, this holds
exactly when $w$ reads $12$ and $13$, in either order, before $15$: a
completion reading $15$ first completes the occurrence $14,15,x$ of $231$
with $x\in\{12,13\}$.  Hence $(\{12,13\},\{15\})$, the stack produced by
the legal moves, is faithful to $\sigma$.  The stack $(\{12,13,15\})$ is
not: its language contains the order $15,12,13$, which violates the
obligation of $9$.  Nor is $(\{12\},\{13\},\{15\})$: its language excludes
the order $13,12,15$, which satisfies every obligation.
\end{example}

\begin{lemma}[Stack merger]\label{lem:merger}
Let $(\sigma,S)$ be a scan state whose stack $S=(I_1,\ldots,I_s)$ is
faithful to $\sigma$, and let $x$ be an unread letter that is a $d$-trigger
of $\sigma x$ and is smaller than every $d$-trigger of $\sigma$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] If $\sigma$ has no $d$-trigger, then $S$ is empty, and the stack
  $(E)$, where $E$ is the set of unread letters larger than $x$, is faithful
  to $\sigma x$; it is the empty stack when $E=\varnothing$.
\item[(b)] If $\sigma$ has a $d$-trigger, let $q$ be the least one and let
  $E$ be the set of unread letters strictly between $x$ and $q$.  Then $E$
  is empty or an interval of the unread alphabet; if $s\geq1$, then $E<I_1$
  and $E\cup I_1$ is an interval of the unread alphabet; and the merged
  stack
  \[
    (E\cup I_1,I_2,\ldots,I_s),
  \]
  or $(E)$ when $s=0$, with an empty set deleted, is faithful to $\sigma x$.
  In particular only the active head changes.  The old tail
  $I_2,\ldots,I_s$ is unchanged.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We use repeatedly that a direct sum of $231$-avoiding words avoids $231$,
since positions and values both increase from each block to the next.
Let $w$ be a completion of $\sigma x$.  Then $xw$ is a completion of
$\sigma$, and since $x$ is smaller than every $d$-trigger of $\sigma$, the
words $w$ and $xw$ satisfy the same residual obligations of the
$d$-triggers of $\sigma$.  Moreover $x$ lies in no stack interval: if
$\sigma$ has no $d$-trigger, the stack is empty by \cref{def:scan-state},
and otherwise the stack consists of unread letters larger than the least
$d$-trigger $q$ of $\sigma$, while $x<q$.  Hence $xw$ and $w$ have the same
restriction to the stack values.
The residual obligations of the $d$-triggers of $\sigma x$ are those of
$\sigma$ together with the obligation of $x$, which requires
$w|_{\{y:y>x\}}$ to avoid $231$.  Moreover, $x$ is the least $d$-trigger of
$\sigma x$.

(a) If $\sigma$ has no $d$-trigger, then $S$ is empty by \cref{def:scan-state},
and $x$ is the only $d$-trigger of $\sigma x$.  The unread letters larger than
$x$ form $E$, and the residual obligations of $\sigma x$ reduce to the
obligation of $x$, that $w|_E$ avoid $231$.  Hence $(E)$ is faithful to
$\sigma x$.

(b) By \cref{lem:legal-moves}(a), the merged stack together with $\sigma x$
is a scan state, and its union is the set of unread letters above $x$.
Since $I_1\cup\cdots\cup I_s$ is the whole set of unread
letters above $q$ and $I_1<\cdots<I_s$, each $I_j$ is an interval of the
unread alphabet.  Any unread value between two values of $E$ lies strictly
between $x$ and $q$, hence in $E$, so $E$ is an interval too.  Every value
of $E$ is smaller than $q$, while every stack value is larger than $q$.
Hence $E<I_1$ when $s\geq1$.  No unread value lies strictly between $E$ and
$I_1$: it would lie either strictly between $x$ and $q$, hence in $E$, or
above $q$, hence in the stack.  Hence $E\cup I_1$ is an interval of the
unread alphabet.  Reading $x$, which lies below $E$, does not change this.

It remains to prove faithfulness: that $w$ satisfies the residual
obligations of $\sigma x$ exactly when its restriction to the merged stack
lies in the corresponding direct sum.  If $s=0$, faithfulness of the empty
stack to $\sigma$ says that every completion of $\sigma$ satisfies the
residual obligations of all $d$-triggers of $\sigma$, and no unread value
exceeds $q$.  Hence the condition on $w$ is the obligation of
$x$ on $E$ alone, as in (a).  Assume $s\geq1$.  If $E=\varnothing$, the
obligation of $x$ asks for $231$-avoidance on $I_1\cup\cdots\cup I_s$, which
the old condition already implies by the direct-sum fact.  Hence the old stack
is unchanged and is still faithful to $\sigma x$.  Assume $E\neq\varnothing$.
If $s=1$, the obligation of $x$ is $231$-avoidance on $E\cup I_1$, which is
the claimed condition, and it implies the old condition on the subword
$I_1$.  Assume $s\geq2$.

Necessity.  Suppose $w$ satisfies the residual obligations of $\sigma x$.
The old condition places every value of $I_1$ before every value of $I_2$,
and hence before every later interval, and makes $w|_{I_j}$ avoid $231$ for
every $j$.  The obligation of $x$ makes $w|_{E\cup I_1}$ avoid $231$.  If
some $z\in I_2$ occurred before some $e\in E$, choose any $y\in I_1$: the
old ordering puts $y$ before $z$, so $y,z,e\sim231$ in $w|_{\{y:y>x\}}$,
contradicting the obligation of $x$.  The same argument applies to every
later interval, so all of $E\cup I_1$ precedes the old tail, and the
restriction of $w$ to the merged stack lies in the merged direct sum.

Sufficiency.  Suppose the restriction of $w$ to the merged stack lies in
the merged direct sum.  Its restriction to $I_1\cup\cdots\cup I_s$ then lies
in the old direct sum, so $w$ satisfies the residual obligations of the
$d$-triggers of $\sigma$.  Moreover, $w|_{\{y:y>x\}}$ is a direct sum of
$231$-avoiding words, so it avoids $231$, which is the obligation of $x$.
\end{proof}

The merger creates no separation between $E$ and $I_1$, although read
letters lie between them in value: the old least trigger $q$ and the
earlier triggers above $q$, among them letters read from earlier active
heads.  When $d\geq2$, earlier letters that were not triggers may lie
inside the value range of $E$ as well, and they create no separation
either.  All of these letters were read before the new trigger, so none
belongs to its projection, and no earlier obligation involves $E$, since
every earlier trigger is at least $q$.  A separation produced by a split is
therefore not permanent: once the interval below it has been exhausted, a
later merger can extend the head past the letters that produced it, as
rows $2$--$5$ of \cref{ex:1342-scan} show.
\Cref{lem:1342-separators,cor:separators} determine the separations of the
stack exactly.

\begin{example}[One merger]\label{ex:one-merger}
Continue \cref{ex:one-projection}.  The fifth letter $5$ of $\pi$ is a
trigger smaller than the earlier triggers $9,11,10,14$.  In
\cref{lem:merger}(b), $q=9$ and $E=\{6,7,8\}$, the unread values between $5$
and $9$.  The current stack $\{12,13\}\mid\{15\}$ consists exactly of the
unread values above $9$, and the stack is faithful to the prefix by
\cref{prop:scan-states}(a) below, since $9,11,10,14$ is a legal prefix.
The merged stack is
\[
 \{6,7,8,12,13\}\mid\{15\}.
\]
The values $6,7,8$ join the head without a separation: the read letters
$9$, $10$, $11$, which lie between them and $12$ in value, were read before
the trigger $5$ and constrain nothing in $E$.  They must, however, precede
$15$: if $15$ came before $6$, then either the old obligation is already
violated, or $12$ precedes $15$ and $12,15,6\sim231$ in the projection of
$5$, that is, $5,12,15,6\sim1342$.  Inside the head only $231$-avoidance
remains.  The permutation $\pi$ continues with $12$ before $6$, and
$13,12,6,7,8$ would have been allowed as well.  For $d=2$ the same permutation is scanned in
\cref{ex:full-state}: there the first $2$-trigger is $11$ rather than $9$,
and the corresponding merger occurs at the $2$-trigger $6$, with $q=10$ and
$E=\{7,8\}$.
\end{example}

\begin{proposition}[Scan states of avoiders]\label{prop:scan-states}
Let $\sigma$ be a legal word with stack $\mathcal S(\sigma)=(I_1,\ldots,I_s)$,
and let $q$ be the least $d$-trigger of $\sigma$ when $\sigma$ has one.  The
pair $(\sigma,\mathcal S(\sigma))$ is a scan state, by induction along the
legal moves from the initial state using \cref{lem:legal-moves}(a).
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] The stack $\mathcal S(\sigma)$ is faithful to $\sigma$: a
  completion $w$ of $\sigma$ satisfies the residual obligations of all
  $d$-triggers of $\sigma$ if and only if
  \[
    w|_{I_1\cup\cdots\cup I_s}\in
    \Av(231)(I_1)\oplus\cdots\oplus\Av(231)(I_s),
  \]
  where the right-hand side is the empty word when $s=0$.
\item[(b)] If reading the unread letter $x$ from $\sigma$ is illegal, then no
  $\beta_d$-avoiding permutation has the prefix $\sigma x$.
\item[(c)] $\mathcal R(\sigma)$ is the set of $\beta_d$-avoiding permutations
  of $[n]$ with prefix $\sigma$.  In particular
  $\mathcal R(\emptyword)=\Av_n(\beta_d)$,
  and a word is legal if and only if it is a prefix of a $\beta_d$-avoiding
  permutation.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We prove (a) by induction along $\sigma$.  For the empty word there is no
trigger, no obligation and no stack, so the empty stack is faithful.  Let
$S=\mathcal S(\sigma)$ be faithful to $\sigma$, let $x$ be a legal move, and
consider $\sigma x$ in the three cases of \cref{def:scan-state}.

In a merger, $x$ is a $d$-trigger of $\sigma x$ smaller than every
$d$-trigger of $\sigma$, and by the induction hypothesis $S$ is faithful to
$\sigma$.  The stack $S'$ of \cref{def:scan-state}(a) is the stack of
\cref{lem:merger}(a) or (b) according as $\sigma$ has no $d$-trigger or has
one, so $S'$ is faithful to $\sigma x$.

In a split, $x\in I_1$, so $x>q$ and $x$ is a $d$-trigger of $\sigma x$ as
noted after \cref{def:scan-state}.  A completion $w$ of $\sigma x$ satisfies
the residual obligations of the triggers of $\sigma$ if and only if the
completion $xw$ of $\sigma$ does, that is, by the induction hypothesis, if
and only if $(xw)|_{I_1}\in\Av(231)(I_1)$, $w|_{I_j}\in\Av(231)(I_j)$ for
$j\geq2$, and $w$ reads the sets $I_1\setminus\{x\},I_2,\ldots,I_s$ in this
order.  By \cref{lem:first-letter}, the first condition holds if and only if
$w|_{I_1\setminus\{x\}}\in\Av(231)(I_1^{<x})\oplus\Av(231)(I_1^{>x})$.
Together, these conditions say that the restriction of $w$ to the stack
values lies in the direct sum of the split stack.  The obligation of the new
trigger $x$ adds nothing: its projection consists of the unread letters
above $x$, namely $I_1^{>x}\cup I_2\cup\cdots\cup I_s$, on which the split
stack already prescribes a direct sum of $231$-avoiding words, and such a
word avoids $231$ because positions and values both increase from each
block to the next.

In a non-trigger move, $x$ is not a $d$-trigger of $\sigma x$.  If $\sigma$
has no $d$-trigger, there is no obligation and no stack before or after the
move.  Otherwise $x<q$, as in the proof of \cref{lem:legal-moves}, so $x$
belongs to no projection of a $d$-trigger of $\sigma$, every projection
consisting of letters larger than its trigger, which is at least $q$.  The
residual obligations and the stack are therefore unchanged, and $S$ remains
faithful to $\sigma x$.

For (b), let $x$ be illegal.  By \cref{lem:legal-moves}(b), $x$ lies in a
deferred interval $I_j$, $j\geq2$.  Every completion of $\sigma$ beginning
with $x$ reads $x$ before the letters of the nonempty set $I_1$, so its
restriction to the stack values is not in the direct sum of (a), and by (a)
it violates the residual obligation of some $d$-trigger $c$ of $\sigma$: the projection created by $c$ contains $231$, and the permutation
contains $\beta_d$ by \cref{lem:trigger}.

For (c), let $\pi$ be a permutation with prefix $\sigma$.  If $\pi$ avoids
$\beta_d$, then every prefix of $\pi$ is legal: the empty prefix is, and if
a prefix $\sigma'$ of $\pi$ is legal and the next letter $x$ of $\pi$ were
illegal, (b) would forbid the prefix $\sigma'x$ of the avoider $\pi$.  Hence
$\pi\in\mathcal R(\sigma)$.  Conversely, let $\pi\in\mathcal R(\sigma)$.  Then $\pi$ itself
is legal, no letter is unread, the stack is empty, and by (a) the
empty completion satisfies the residual obligation of every $d$-trigger of
$\pi$.  That is, the projection of every $d$-trigger avoids $231$ after
standardization, and $\pi$ avoids $\beta_d$ by \cref{lem:trigger}.  This
proves the first sentence of (c) and, with $\sigma=\emptyword$, that
$\mathcal R(\emptyword)=\Av_n(\beta_d)$.  A prefix of an avoider is legal by the
first part.  Conversely, a legal $\sigma$ has an avoiding completion: read
the unread letters smaller than $q$, or all unread letters when $\sigma$ has
no $d$-trigger, in decreasing order, and then the intervals $I_1,\ldots,I_s$
in order, each in decreasing order.  Each letter of the first group is the
largest unread letter below the least trigger, or the largest unread letter
when there is none, so it is either not a $d$-trigger or a new least
$d$-trigger with $E=\varnothing$.  In both cases the move is legal and the
stack is unchanged.  Each later letter is the greatest element of the
active head, a $d$-trigger exceeding $q$ as noted after \cref{def:scan-state}, so reading it is an endpoint choice.  All moves are
legal, so the completed permutation lies in $\mathcal R(\sigma)$ and avoids
$\beta_d$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The one-threshold state}

For $d=1$, every entry is a trigger, so the least $d$-trigger $q$ of
\cref{def:scan-state} is the least letter $m$ read so far.  Before the first
letter, $m$ is a virtual value larger than every letter.  A legal move is a
new left-to-right minimum, which adjoins the unread values between it and
$m$ to the active head or creates the stack when the stack is empty, or a
letter of the active head, which shortens the head at an endpoint choice
and replaces it by its lower and upper subintervals at an interior choice.
By \cref{def:scan-state,lem:legal-moves}, the stack of a legal prefix
consists of the unread letters above $m$, and by \cref{prop:scan-states}(c) the legal
prefixes are the prefixes of $1342$-avoiding permutations.  The following lemma determines the
separations of the stack.  Two unread values are \emph{adjacent} if no unread value lies
strictly between them.  By a \emph{prefix} of a $1342$-avoiding permutation
we mean an initial segment of one.  This is stronger than avoiding $1342$,
since $134$ avoids $1342$ but, as a prefix of a permutation of $[4]$, its
only completion is $1342$.

\begin{lemma}[Separations of the one-threshold stack]\label{lem:1342-separators}
After a legal prefix has been read (equivalently, by
\cref{prop:scan-states}(c), a prefix of a $1342$-avoiding permutation), let
$u<v$ be adjacent unread letters above $m$.  Then $u$ and $v$ lie in
different intervals of the stack if and only if some letter $x$ with $u<x<v$ was read
after a letter smaller than $u$, that is, at a time when the minimum read
so far was already below $u$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We induct along the scan.  For the empty prefix there is nothing to prove.
The next legal letter is a new minimum or a letter of the active head.
Suppose first that the next letter $x$ is a new minimum, adjoining $E$ to
the head or creating the stack $(E)$.  An adjacent pair $u<v$ above the new
minimum that was not an adjacent pair above the old minimum has $u\in E$,
so $u$ is smaller than the old minimum and hence smaller than every letter
read before $x$.  Every letter between $u$ and $v$ was read before $x$, so
no letter between them was read after a letter smaller than $u$.  Moreover
$v$ lies in $E$ or is the least element of $I_1$, and $E\cup I_1$, or $E$
when the old stack was empty, is a single interval of the updated stack.
Thus neither condition holds for such a pair.  Every other adjacent pair
lies above $x$ and keeps its members, its intervals, and the letters
between them, so both conditions are unchanged for it.  Suppose instead
that $x$ is read from the active head $I_1$.  Only the unread neighbours
$u<x<v$ of $x$ can form a new adjacent pair, and only when both exist and
lie above $m$.  This excludes $x=\min I_1$, where $u$ lies below $m$.  For
an interior choice, $u$ and $v$ lie in the two parts created by the split.
For $x=\max I_1$ with $|I_1|\geq2$, $u$ lies in the shortened head and $v$
is the least element of $I_2$ if a deferred interval remains.  Otherwise
$x$ is the largest unread letter and no new pair arises.  In every case $u$ and
$v$ lie in different intervals, and $x$, read after $m<u$, witnesses the
second condition.  Every other adjacent pair, and every letter between its
members, is unchanged.
\end{proof}

Hence the stack is recovered from the scanned prefix alone: its values are
the unread letters above $m$, and its intervals are the blocks into which
this increasing list is cut at exactly the adjacent pairs of the lemma.
The precedence recorded by a separation is also visible directly: if $x$
was read after $c<u$ and $v$ preceded $u$, then $c,x,v,u$ would form
$1342$.  Conversely, two letters of the same interval can occur in either
order in a $1342$-avoiding completion.  \Cref{cor:separators} proves this
and extends both statements to every $d$.  Adjacency cannot be dropped:
after the prefix $3,5,1$ of a $1342$-avoiding permutation of $[7]$ the
stack is $\{2,4\}\mid\{6,7\}$, so $2$ and $6$ lie in different intervals,
although no letter between them was read after a letter smaller than $2$.

\begin{example}[A one-threshold scan]\label{ex:1342-scan}
We scan the permutation $\pi$ of \eqref{eq:running-example} completely.
The table lists, after each letter $\pi_i$, the type of move, the current
minimum $m$, the interval stack, and the symbol $W_p(L)$ of
\cref{sec:one-threshold}, where $p$ is the number of unread letters below
$m$, $L$ lists the interval sizes, and $W_p(L)$ is the number of
$1342$-avoiding completions of the prefix.  Every new minimum is a merger
in the sense of \cref{def:scan-state}(a).  The table writes ``merger'' when

\subsection{The exact one-threshold recurrence}\label{sec:one-threshold}

Recall that $m$ is the least letter in the scanned prefix, virtual for the
empty prefix.  Let $p$ be the number of unread letters below $m$, which we
call the \emph{control} of the state, and let $L=(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_s)$ be
the positive sizes of the active and deferred intervals.  For a legal prefix with these data, let $W_p(L)$ be the number
of completions that produce a $1342$-avoiding permutation.  By
\cref{prop:scan-states}(c), these completions are exactly the sequences of
legal moves that read every unread letter, and by \cref{def:scan-state} the
legal moves from a legal prefix, their successor states and their
multiplicities depend only on $(p,L)$ and on the local ranks of the letters
read.  Hence this number depends only on $(p,L)$, as the proof below makes
explicit.
Write $|L|=\sum_{i=1}^s\ell_i$ and
$\operatorname{len}(L)=s$.  Let $\nz(x_1,\ldots,x_r)$ denote the list
obtained by deleting every zero entry.  If $L'$ is a list, $(\ell)L'$ and
$(a,b)L'$ denote concatenation with the displayed initial entries.  For a
statement $E$, $\one_E$ denotes its indicator.

\begin{proposition}[One-threshold recurrence]\label{prop:scalar-literal}
For $L=(\ell)L'$ with $\ell\geq1$,
\begin{align}
W_p((\ell)L')={}&\sum_{h=0}^{p-1}
 W_h\!\left((\ell+p-1-h)L'\right)+E_\ell(p,L') \label{eq:W}\\
&+\sum_{\substack{a,b\geq1\\a+b=\ell-1}}W_p((a,b)L'), \notag
\end{align}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:W-endpoint}
 E_\ell(p,L')=
 \begin{cases}
  W_p(L'),&\ell=1,\\
  2W_p((\ell-1)L'),&\ell\geq2.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The boundary and initial conditions are
\begin{align}
 W_p(\varnothing)&=\one_{p=0}+
  \sum_{h=0}^{p-1}W_h\!\left(\nz(p-1-h)\right),
  \label{eq:W-boundary}\\
 |\Av_n(1342)|&=W_n(\varnothing). \label{eq:W-initial}
\end{align}
These equations count every $1342$-avoiding permutation exactly once.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{prop:scan-states}(c), the completions that produce a
$1342$-avoiding permutation are exactly those all of whose moves are legal,
and by \cref{def:scan-state} with \cref{lem:legal-moves}(b) the legal moves
from a legal prefix are the new minima and the letters of the active head.
Partition the possible next letters by their location.  If the next letter
is below $m$, let $h$ be the number of unread letters below it.  There is one
letter for each $0\leq h<p$.  The new minimum leaves $h$ unrestricted lower
letters, while the $p-1-h$ letters between it and $m$ join the active
interval.  This gives the first line of \eqref{eq:W}.  Among constrained
letters, only the active interval can be read legally, by
\cref{lem:legal-moves}(b).  If the active interval's size is one, it has
one endpoint.  If its size is at least two, its two endpoints have the same
standardized successor.  These choices give \eqref{eq:W-endpoint}.  An
interior letter replaces the active interval by ordered subintervals $(a,b)$
with $a,b\geq1$ and $a+b=\ell-1$, giving the last line of \eqref{eq:W} by
\cref{lem:first-letter}.  With an empty stack only a new minimum can be read, which gives
\eqref{eq:W-boundary}.  Regard each coefficient-$1$ summand as
one transition and the coefficient-$2$ summand as two labeled transitions,
of local ranks $1$ and $\ell$.

The quantity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rho-scalar}
 \rho_1(p,L)=p+|L|
\end{equation}
decreases by one under every transition.  Thus every maximal transition
sequence ends at $(0,\varnothing)$.  Conversely, the selected local rank at
each step recovers a unique next letter in the current ordered alphabet.
Maximal transition sequences and avoiding completions are therefore inverse constructions,
proving correctness and \eqref{eq:W-initial}.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[All one-threshold moves]\label{ex:1342-state}
After the sixth letter of \eqref{eq:running-example}, the unread letters
below the current minimum $5$ are $1,2,3,4$, and the interval stack is
$\{6,7,8\}\mid\{13\}\mid\{15\}$.  The state is $W_4((3,1,1))$, and
\eqref{eq:W} gives
\begin{align*}
 W_4((3,1,1))={}&W_0((6,1,1))+W_1((5,1,1))+W_2((4,1,1))+W_3((3,1,1))\\
 &+2W_4((2,1,1))+W_4((1,1,1,1)).
\end{align*}
The first four terms correspond to reading $1$, $2$, $3$, or $4$, which
adjoin $\{2,3,4\}$, $\{3,4\}$, $\{4\}$, or nothing to the active interval.
The fifth term records the two endpoints $6,8$ of the active interval, and
the last records its interior letter $7$.  Neither $13$ nor $15$ can be
read legally until the active interval is empty.  The scan of
\cref{ex:1342-scan} continues with the endpoint $6$.
\end{example}

\subsection{The exponential literal state space}

The recurrence \eqref{eq:W} is finite for each $n$, but memoizing its
composition-valued argument does not give a polynomial bound.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:scalar-exponential}
For $n\geq2$, the computation of $W_n(\varnothing)$ reaches at least
$F_{n-1}$ distinct composition arguments, where $F_{n-1}$ is the $(n-1)$st
Fibonacci number, $F_1=F_2=1$.  Consequently,
literal memoization of \eqref{eq:W} has exponentially many states.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Choosing the letter $1$ at the initial state reaches $W_0((n-1))$.  Repeated
interior choices reach every positive composition $L$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scalar-fib-comps}
 |L|+\operatorname{len}(L)=n.
\end{equation}
Indeed, reversing an interior split replaces
$(a,b,\ldots)$ by $(a+b+1,\ldots)$, and repeated reversals return every such
composition to $(n-1)$.  If $L$ has $s$ parts, then $|L|=n-s$, so the number
of choices is $\binom{n-s-1}{s-1}$.  Summing over
$1\leq s\leq\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ gives
\[
 \sum_{s=1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\binom{n-s-1}{s-1}=F_{n-1}.\qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

The values of the ordered parts cannot be replaced by their total and their
number.  For example, when $p=0$, \eqref{eq:stack-form} gives
$W_0((1,3))=C_1C_3=5$ and $W_0((2,2))=C_2^2=4$, where
$C_j=\frac1{j+1}\binom{2j}{j}$.  The next construction removes the entire
composition from the memoization key while retaining its effect on completion
counts.

\subsection{Protected-tail factorization}

Before the general statement, consider the smallest nontrivial instance of
\eqref{eq:W}: one unread letter below the current minimum and a head of size
one, with an arbitrary tail $L'$.  Either the head letter is read first, which exposes $L'$ at
control $1$, or the lower letter is read first and the head letter next,
which exposes $L'$ at control $0$.  Hence
\[
  W_1((1)L')=W_1(L')+W_0(L').
\]
The two coefficients count the ways to reach the exposure of $L'$ at
controls $1$ and $0$, and they do not depend on $L'$.  Writing them as
$K_1(1,1)=K_1(1,0)=1$, the identity reads
$W_1((1)L')=\sum_tK_1(1,t)W_t(L')$.  The theorem below shows systematically
when such a factorization holds for a recurrence on a control set and a
stack: the transitions before the tail is exposed must leave the tail
unchanged and must not depend on it.

Let $\mathcal C$ be a set and $\mathcal B^*$ the set of finite words over an
alphabet $\mathcal B$.  In the application below, an element of $\mathcal C$
records the number of unread values that the obligations so far leave
unconstrained, and a word in $\mathcal B^*$ lists the sizes of the pending
intervals, active head first.  A \emph{recurrence} $H$ on
$\mathcal C\times\mathcal B^*$ assigns to each nonboundary state $(c,Y)$ an
equation
\[
 H_c(Y)=\sum_e H_{c_e}(Y_e)
\]
with finitely many summands, a summand with coefficient $m$ being written as
$m$ equal summands, as in the proof of \cref{prop:scalar-literal}.  A
boundary state has a prescribed nonnegative-integer value and no equation.
Each summand is a \emph{transition} $(c,Y)\to(c_e,Y_e)$, so parallel
transitions may occur.  A \emph{path} is a finite sequence of consecutive
transitions, and a \emph{complete path} is one ending at a boundary state.
The recurrence \emph{terminates} if it has no infinite path.  Then every
state has finitely many complete paths, and induction along the
well-founded transition relation shows that $H_c(Y)$ is the sum, over the
complete paths from $(c,Y)$, of the boundary values at their last states.

For a factorization $Y=UL$ of a stack we write $U\mid L$ when the suffix
$L$ is the one under consideration.  The marker is a notational device and
not part of the state.  In the application, $L$ is a tail of the interval
stack at a separation, chosen freely.  A state is an \emph{exposure} of $L$
if its stack is exactly $L$, that is, if it has the form
$(t,\varnothing\mid L)$.  Given a state $s_0=(c,U\mid L)$ with
$U\ne\varnothing$, call $L$ \emph{protected before exposure from $s_0$} if
the following two conditions hold.
(P1) Every state reachable from $s_0$ by a path containing no exposure of
$L$ has the form $(c',U'\mid L)$ with $U'\ne\varnothing$.
(P2) For every such state and every word $L^\star\in\mathcal B^*$,
replacing $L$ by $L^\star$ induces a bijection between the transitions
leaving $(c',U'\mid L)$ and those leaving $(c',U'\mid L^\star)$, with
corresponding targets $(c'',V'\mid L)$ and $(c'',V'\mid L^\star)$.
Thus (P1) says that the suffix $L$ survives untouched until it is exposed,
and (P2) says that the transitions available before exposure do not depend
on it.  Call $L$ \emph{necessarily exposed from $s_0$} if every complete
path from $s_0$ contains an exposure of $L$, possibly as its last state.

\begin{theorem}[Protected-tail factorization]
\label{thm:protected-tail-principle}
Let $H$ be a terminating recurrence on
$\mathcal C\times\mathcal B^*$.  Suppose that, for every
$c\in\mathcal C$, $x\in\mathcal B$, and $L\in\mathcal B^*$, the marked
suffix $L$ is protected before exposure from $(c,x\mid L)$ and necessarily
exposed from that state.

Let $K_x^L(c,t)$ be the number of paths from $(c,x\mid L)$ that are stopped
at their first exposure of $L$ and reach it at $(t,\varnothing\mid L)$.  Then $K_x^L(c,t)$ is independent of $L$.  Denote
it by $K_x(c,t)$.  If $Y=x_1\cdots x_s$ and
$G_t=H_t(\varnothing)$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:abstract-factorization}
 H_c(Y)=\sum_t(K_{x_1}K_{x_2}\cdots K_{x_s})(c,t)G_t.
\end{equation}
Here
\[
 (AB)(c,t)=\sum_{u\in\mathcal C}A(c,u)B(u,t),
\]
and the empty product is the identity kernel $\one_{c=t}$.  All displayed
sums are finite by termination.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By (P1) and (P2) and induction on the number of transitions, replacing $L$
by $L^\star$ gives a bijection between the paths from $(c,x\mid L)$ that
contain no exposure of $L$ and the paths from $(c,x\mid L^\star)$ that
contain no exposure of $L^\star$, with equal controls at corresponding
states.  The transition that produces the first exposure leaves a state of
the form $(c',U'\mid L)$ and is matched as well.  Hence $K_x^L(c,t)$ is
independent of $L$.  By necessary exposure, every complete path from
$(c,x\mid L)$ has a unique first exposure of $L$, of the form
$(t,\varnothing\mid L)$.  Cutting the path there gives a stopped path
counted by $K_x(c,t)$ followed by a complete path from $(t,L)$, counted by
$H_t(L)$.  Concatenation is the inverse operation.  Therefore
\[
 H_c(xL)=\sum_tK_x(c,t)H_t(L).
\]
Applying this identity successively to $x_1,\ldots,x_s$ proves
\eqref{eq:abstract-factorization}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Pushdown systems and pop sequences]
\label{rem:pop-sequences}
Conditions (P1) and (P2) say that the recurrence is a pushdown system: a
transition depends only on the control and on the part of the stack before
the marker, and the part after it is untouched until exposed, which a
pushdown automaton satisfies by definition.  The kernel $K_x(c,t)$ counts
the runs that pop $x$ while passing from $c$ to $t$, and
\cref{thm:protected-tail-principle} is the counting form of the classical
conversion of a pushdown automaton into a context-free grammar, whose
variable $[c\,x\,t]$ describes exactly those runs
\cite[Theorem~6.14]{HopcroftMotwaniUllman2001},
\cite[Lemma~2.27]{Sipser2013}.  The same runs are the pop sequences of
weighted pushdown systems \cite{RepsSchwoonJhaMelski2005}, and
\eqref{eq:abstract-factorization} is the first-passage decomposition of
weighted lattice paths \cite[Sections~I.5.3 and~V.4]{FlajoletSedgewick2009}.
We state and prove the theorem because the control set and the alphabet are
infinite in our application, so the textbook statements do not apply
verbatim, and because the consequence differs: with infinitely many
controls the construction yields an infinite grammar, so no algebraicity
follows from it \cite{ChomskySchutzenberger1963}, and what the
factorization provides is a memoization key of polynomial size
(\cref{thm:main}) rather than a generating function.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The scalar kernel algorithm}

We apply \cref{thm:protected-tail-principle} to the recurrence \eqref{eq:W},
with $\mathcal C=\N$ the set of values of $p$, with $\mathcal B$ the set of
positive integers, and with $W$ in the role of the recurrence $H$.
A word in $\mathcal B^*$ is then the list $L$ of interval sizes.  Mark the
boundary before an arbitrary old
tail $L$.  A new minimum can enlarge the active head, an endpoint can shorten
it, and an interior letter can replace it by two new parts.  None of these
moves changes $L$ before every part preceding the boundary has disappeared.
Equations \eqref{eq:W} and \eqref{eq:W-endpoint} show that, before exposure,
the transitions leaving a state and their targets are determined by $p$ and
the blocks preceding the marker.  Replacing $L$ therefore gives the
bijection of outgoing transitions required by (P2).  Every move decreases
\eqref{eq:rho-scalar}, and the only terminal state has an empty stack.  Hence
no complete path can terminate before exposing $L$.  Thus
\cref{thm:protected-tail-principle} applies.

For $\ell\geq1$, let $K_\ell(p,t)$ be the number of paths from
$W_p((\ell)\mid L)$ stopped when $L$ is first exposed, at
$W_t(\varnothing\mid L)$.  The definition
is independent of $L$.  Recall that the control $p$ is the number of unread
letters below the current minimum.  In the kernel we call $p$ the
\emph{source control}, $t$ a \emph{terminal control}, and
$K_\ell(p,\mathord\cdot)$ a \emph{scalar kernel row}.  A transition
that selects an unread letter below the current minimum is a \emph{base
move}.  A transition that selects a value from the active head is an
\emph{active-interval move}.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scalar-factorization}
 W_p((\ell)L)=\sum_{t\geq0}K_\ell(p,t)W_t(L).
\end{equation}
Put $K_0(p,t)=\one_{p=t}$ and $G_p=W_p(\varnothing)$.

\begin{proposition}[Scalar kernel recurrences]
\label{prop:scalar-kernel-recurrence}
For $\ell\geq1$,
\begin{align}
K_\ell(p,t)={}&\sum_{h=0}^{p-1}K_{\ell+p-1-h}(h,t)
 +D_\ell(p,t) \label{eq:scalar-K}\\
&+\sum_{\substack{a,b\geq1\\a+b=\ell-1}}
  \sum_{u\geq0}K_a(p,u)K_b(u,t), \notag
\end{align}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scalar-D}
 D_1(p,t)=\one_{p=t},
 \qquad D_\ell(p,t)=2K_{\ell-1}(p,t)\quad(\ell\geq2).
\end{equation}
The empty-stack values satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scalar-G}
 G_p=\one_{p=0}+
 \sum_{h=0}^{p-1}\sum_{t\geq0}K_{p-1-h}(h,t)G_t,
 \qquad |\Av_n(1342)|=G_n.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Partition a stopped path by its first move.  A new minimum leaving $h$ lower
letters gives the first sum in \eqref{eq:scalar-K}.  If $\ell=1$, consuming
the only active letter exposes the old tail at the same control.  If
$\ell\geq2$, either endpoint leaves an active interval of size $\ell-1$.
These are the terms in \eqref{eq:scalar-D}.  An interior letter creates
ordered parts $(a,b)$ with $a+b=\ell-1$.  Re-mark the resulting state as
$(p,(a)\mid(b)L)$.  A path stopped at the first exposure of $L$ passes
through a unique first exposure of $(b)L$, because every transition changes
the stack only in its current active head.  Cutting there splits the path into one
counted by $K_a(p,u)$, for some intermediate control $u$, and one counted by
$K_b(u,t)$.  Concatenation inverts the cut.  Summing over $u$ gives the last
line of \eqref{eq:scalar-K}.  Finally, apply \eqref{eq:W-boundary}.  A new
minimum creates a part of size $p-1-h$, to which
\eqref{eq:scalar-factorization} applies, and
$K_0$ covers the case $h=p-1$.  This proves \eqref{eq:scalar-G}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Exact scalar support]\label{lem:scalar-support}
For every $p\geq0$ and $\ell\geq1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scalar-support}
 \supp K_\ell(p,\mathord\cdot)=\{0,1,\ldots,p\}.
\end{equation}
Moreover, $K_\ell(p,p)=C_\ell$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A base move strictly decreases the control, while an active-interval move leaves it
unchanged.  Thus every terminal control is at most $p$.  A path ending at
$p$ uses no base move.  By \cref{lem:first-letter}, such paths are in
bijection with the $231$-avoiding permutations of the original
$\ell$-element interval, and hence are counted by $C_\ell$.

For $t<p$, first choose the unique base letter having exactly $t$ unread
base letters below it.  The control becomes $t$ and the active interval has
size $\ell+p-1-t$.  Repeatedly selecting an endpoint exhausts that interval
without selecting another base letter and exposes the marked suffix at
control $t$.
Thus every $t\in\{0,\ldots,p\}$ occurs.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[From kernel rows to $|\Av_5(1342)|$]
\label{ex:scalar-kernel}
Order each row by terminal control.  Equations
\eqref{eq:scalar-K}--\eqref{eq:scalar-D} give
\[
 K_1(1,\mathord\cdot)=(1,1),\quad
 K_3(0,\mathord\cdot)=(5),\quad
 K_2(1,\mathord\cdot)=(4,2),\quad
 K_1(2,\mathord\cdot)=(3,1,1),
\]
for example
$K_1(2,\mathord\cdot)=K_2(0,\mathord\cdot)+K_1(1,\mathord\cdot)+\mathbf e_2
=(2)+(1,1)+\mathbf e_2=(3,1,1)$, where $\mathbf e_t(u)=\one_{u=t}$.  The
rows of grade $4$ follow:
\begin{align*}

  +K_1(0,0)K_2(0,\mathord\cdot)+K_2(0,0)K_1(0,\mathord\cdot)=(10)+(2)+(2)=(14),\\

  +\textstyle\sum_uK_1(1,u)K_1(u,\mathord\cdot)=(5)+(8,4)+(2,1)=(15,5),\\
 K_2(2,\mathord\cdot)&=K_3(0,\mathord\cdot)+K_2(1,\mathord\cdot)

\end{align*}

the five groups corresponding, in order, to $h=0,1,2,3,4$, the last through

\begin{theorem}[Polynomial enumeration of $1342$-avoiders]
\label{thm:scalar-algorithm}
For every $N\geq0$, the numbers
$|\Av_n(1342)|$ for $0\leq n\leq N$ can be computed using $O(N^5)$
exact-integer arithmetic operations and $O(N^3)$ stored integers.  Every
integer has $O(N\log N)$ bits.  Hence the bit time is
$O(N^5M(N\log N))$ and the bit storage is $O(N^4\log N)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Order kernel rows by the \emph{grade} $p+\ell$.  The row in a base term of
\eqref{eq:scalar-K} has grade
$h+(\ell+p-1-h)=p+\ell-1$.  In a split term, $p+a<p+\ell$.  If
$K_a(p,u)\neq0$, then $u\leq p$ by \cref{lem:scalar-support}, and hence
$u+b\leq p+b<p+\ell$.  The term $D_1$ is initial data, and $D_\ell$ for
$\ell\geq2$ uses the row of grade $p+\ell-1$.  Thus increasing grade
is a topological order for all kernel dependencies.  Equation
\eqref{eq:scalar-G} is then evaluated in increasing $p$, because every
terminal control $t$ in its nonzero terms satisfies
$t\leq h<p$.

We compute every row in the \emph{padded region} $p\geq0$, $\ell\geq1$,
$p+\ell\leq N$: it is closed under the dependencies in the preceding
paragraph and contains every row with $\ell\geq1$ needed to evaluate
\eqref{eq:scalar-G} for $n\leq N$, together with rows that no such
evaluation reaches.  It contains
$\binom{N+1}{2}=O(N^2)$ source rows.  By
\cref{lem:scalar-support}, these rows have at most
\[
 \sum_{\ell=1}^N\sum_{p=0}^{N-\ell}(p+1)
 =\binom{N+2}{3}=O(N^3)
\]
nonzero entries in total.  For one source row, a direct sparse evaluation
of all split terms has $O(N)$ choices of $a$, $O(N)$ possible intermediate
controls $u$, and at most $O(N)$ terminal controls in the row being added.  It
therefore uses $O(N^3)$ operations per source row and $O(N^5)$ operations in
total.  The nonsplit kernel terms use $O(N^4)$ operations in total, and
\eqref{eq:scalar-G} uses $O(N^3)$.

For a scalar kernel entry, suffix independence permits us to take the marked
suffix to be empty.  Its initial number of unread letters is then
$p+\ell\leq N$.  An empty-stack computation also starts with at most $N$
unread letters.  This number decreases at every edge.  At each state,
the sum of the outgoing transition multiplicities is the number of legal
next letters and is at most $N$.  Thus each stored count is
at most $(N+1)^N$ and has $O(N\log N)$ bits.  Accounting for integer
multiplication and for the bit length of every stored entry gives the stated
bit bounds.  Every product and partial accumulator is a nonnegative
contribution to a final kernel or empty-stack value, so the same bound applies
to intermediate integers.
\end{proof}

B\'ona proved the algebraic generating function \cite{Bona1997}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bona-1342}
 \sum_{n\geq0}|\Av_n(1342)|x^n
 =\frac{(1-8x)^{3/2}-8x^2+20x+1}{2(1+x)^3}.
\end{equation}
Thus the scalar ordinary generating function is algebraic.
\Cref{thm:scalar-algorithm} supplies a separate transfer construction that
extends to the family: we replace $p$ by $d$ ordered band counts, while the
interval stack, its merger rule, and the protected-tail theorem remain
unchanged.

\section{From one threshold to \texorpdfstring{$d$}{d} thresholds}
\label{sec:frontier}

After a prefix has been read, let $b_j$ be the smallest final entry of an
increasing subsequence of length $j$ contained in that prefix, for
$1\leq j\leq d$.  When the prefix contains no such subsequence, use the
virtual value $b_j=n+j$.  Then
\[
 b_1<b_2<\cdots<b_d.
\]
We call $b_1,\ldots,b_d$ the \emph{patience-sorting thresholds} of the
prefix.  Every nonvirtual threshold value
has already been read.  We call the unread values below $b_d$ the
\emph{base values}.  They fall into $d$ open bands.  Band $0$ lies below
$b_1$, while band $i$ lies between $b_i$ and $b_{i+1}$ for $1\leq i<d$.
Write
\[
 \mathbf p=(p_0,p_1,\ldots,p_{d-1})
\]
for the band sizes.  We again call $\mathbf p$ the \emph{control}, write
$\|\mathbf p\|_1=p_0+\cdots+p_{d-1}$ for its \emph{control mass}, and denote
the zero control by
$\mathbf0$.  Let $B_0,\ldots,B_{d-1}$ be the corresponding unread blocks.
Together with the pending intervals, the value order is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ordered-layout}
 B_0<b_1<B_1<b_2<\cdots<b_{d-1}<B_{d-1}<b_d
 <I_1<\cdots<I_s.
\end{equation}
The $b_j$ are read values, not members of the unread blocks.  Empty
blocks are allowed.  As in the one-threshold case (\cref{sec:obligations}), a
base move selects a value from some $B_i$ and an active-interval move
selects a value from $I_1$.  In the terms of \cref{def:scan-state}, a value
of $B_i$ with $i<d-1$ is a non-trigger move, a value of $B_{d-1}$ is a
merger, and a value of $I_1$ is a split (\cref{prop:state-invariant}(b)
below).  By \cref{lem:least-trigger-frontier} below, the least $d$-trigger
$q$ of \cref{def:scan-state} is $b_d$ whenever $b_d$ is nonvirtual.  Initially
$\mathbf p=(n,0,\ldots,0)$.

If the next base value $x$ lies in band $i\geq1$, then $x$ ends an increasing
subsequence of length $i+1$, since $b_i<x$.  For $i=0$, the letter $x$ is an
increasing subsequence of length one.  In either case, $x$ ends no increasing
subsequence of length $i+2$, since that would imply $b_{i+1}<x$, whereas
$x\in B_i$ gives $x<b_{i+1}$.  Thus only
$b_{i+1}$ changes.  A base value creates a new $d$-trigger exactly when it
lies in band $d-1$.  The first $d-1$ bands update one threshold without
creating an obligation.  The last band also creates a $231$-avoidance
obligation.

\begin{lemma}[Least-trigger threshold]\label{lem:least-trigger-frontier}
After a prefix has been read, either $b_d$ is virtual and no $d$-trigger has
been read, or $b_d$ is the smallest value of a $d$-trigger in the prefix.
Consequently, every unread value below $b_d$ belongs to none of the
projections onto larger values created by earlier triggers, and hence to no
obligation created so far.  When $b_d$ is nonvirtual, every
unread value above $b_d$ belongs to the projection created by the
trigger $b_d$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By definition, a nonvirtual $b_d$ is the least possible final value of an
increasing $d$-subsequence in the prefix, and hence is itself a $d$-trigger.
Every other $d$-trigger has value at least $b_d$.  An unread value $x<b_d$
is therefore smaller than every earlier trigger and occurs in none of their
projections onto larger values.  If $b_d$ is nonvirtual and $x>b_d$ is
unread, then $x$ occurs after the trigger $b_d$ and belongs to its projection.
If $b_d$ is virtual, no increasing $d$-subsequence has yet occurred.
\end{proof}

For example, in
\[
 3\,1\,6\,8\,2\,9\,5\,7\,4,
\]
the entries $3,6$ form an increasing $2$-subsequence, and the subsequent
projection onto values larger than $6$ is $8,9,7\sim231$.  Hence
$3,6,8,9,7$ is an occurrence of $\beta_2=12453$.  The interspersed values
$2,5,4$, all smaller than $6$, do not belong to this projection.

\section{The literal family recurrence}\label{sec:literal}

We now express the band updates in terms of their sizes.  For $0\leq i<d-1$ and
$0\leq h<p_i$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:T}
 T_{i,h}(\mathbf p)=
 (p_0,\ldots,p_{i-1},h,
  p_{i+1}+p_i-1-h,p_{i+2},\ldots,p_{d-1}).
\end{equation}
The chosen letter becomes the new threshold $b_{i+1}$.  Then $h$ values
remain in band $i$, and the values above it in the old band join band $i+1$.  For the
last band put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:U}
 U_h(\mathbf p)=(p_0,\ldots,p_{d-2},h),
 \qquad \delta_h=p_{d-1}-1-h.
\end{equation}
For $d=1$, interpret \eqref{eq:U} as $U_h((p_0))=(h)$.
Here the chosen letter is a new least $d$-trigger.  The $\delta_h$ unread
values above it and below the old $b_d$ join the active stack head.

Let $L=(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_s)$ be the positive pending interval sizes,
active head first.  For a legal prefix (\cref{def:scan-state}) with state
$(\mathbf p,L)$, let $H_{\mathbf p}(L)$ be the number of completions that
produce a $\beta_d$-avoiding permutation.  By \cref{prop:state-invariant}(c)
below, this number depends only on $(\mathbf p,L)$.  For an empty stack
interpret $\ell_1=0$ and its tail as empty.

\begin{proposition}[State invariant]\label{prop:state-invariant}
Let $\sigma$ be a legal prefix with thresholds $b_1<\cdots<b_d$, band sizes
$\mathbf p$, and stack $\mathcal S(\sigma)=(I_1,\ldots,I_s)$ with sizes
$L=(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_s)$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] The ordered layout \eqref{eq:ordered-layout} holds: the unread
  values below $b_d$ form the $d$ bands, and $I_1\cup\cdots\cup I_s$ is the
  set of unread values above $b_d$.  A completion $w$ of $\sigma$ satisfies
  the residual obligations of all $d$-triggers of $\sigma$ exactly when
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:obligation-language}
   w\big|_{I_1\cup\cdots\cup I_s}
   \in
   \Av(231)(I_1)\oplus\cdots\oplus\Av(231)(I_s);
  \end{equation}
  the base values are unrestricted by these obligations.
\item[(b)] The legal moves from $\sigma$, and their effect on
  $(\mathbf p,L)$, are the following.  A value of $B_i$ with $i<d-1$ having
  $h$ unread values of $B_i$ below it is a non-trigger move to
  $(T_{i,h}(\mathbf p),L)$.  A value of $B_{d-1}$ having $h$ unread values of
  $B_{d-1}$ below it is a merger to
  $(U_h(\mathbf p),\nz(\ell_1+\delta_h,\ell_2,\ldots))$.  A value of $I_1$
  of local rank $r$ is a split to
  $(\mathbf p,\nz(r-1,\ell_1-r,\ell_2,\ldots))$.  No other letter is legal.
\item[(c)] Consequently, the set of $\beta_d$-avoiding completions of
  $\sigma$ is determined, up to order isomorphism, by $(\mathbf p,L)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(a) By \cref{lem:least-trigger-frontier}, $b_d$ is the least $d$-trigger of
$\sigma$ when it is nonvirtual, and $\sigma$ has no $d$-trigger when $b_d$
is virtual.  Hence, by \cref{def:scan-state}, the stack consists of the
unread values above $b_d$ (no value exceeds a virtual $b_d=n+d$, so
the two descriptions agree), while the unread values below $b_d$ form the
bands by definition.  This is \eqref{eq:ordered-layout}.  The language
description \eqref{eq:obligation-language} is \cref{prop:scan-states}(a),
and the base values lie in no interval.

(b) We classify the unread letters by \cref{def:scan-state}.  Let
$x\in B_i$ with $i<d-1$, and let $h$ be the number of unread values of $B_i$
below $x$.  As observed before \cref{lem:least-trigger-frontier}, $x$ ends
an increasing subsequence of length $i+1$ but none of length $i+2\leq d$, so
$x$ is not a $d$-trigger of $\sigma x$: reading it is a non-trigger move, and
the stack is unchanged.  Replacing $b_{i+1}$ by $x$ leaves $h$ values in
band $i$ and moves the other $p_i-1-h$ values into band $i+1$, giving
$T_{i,h}(\mathbf p)$.  Next let $x\in B_{d-1}$, with $h$ unread values of
$B_{d-1}$ below it.  As observed before
\cref{lem:least-trigger-frontier} with $i=d-1$, the letter $x$ ends an
increasing $d$-subsequence (for $d=1$, $x$ is itself one) and changes only
$b_d$.  Moreover, $x<b_d$ makes it smaller than every $d$-trigger of
$\sigma$ by \cref{lem:least-trigger-frontier}.  Reading it is therefore a merger, with
$q=b_d$ when $b_d$ is nonvirtual.  When $b_d$ is virtual, $\sigma$ has no
$d$-trigger and, by \cref{def:scan-state}(a), $E$ is the set of all unread
values above $x$, again the unread values strictly between $x$ and
$b_d=n+d$.  In both cases $E$ has $\delta_h=p_{d-1}-1-h$ elements, $x$ becomes the new $b_d$ with $h$ values left in the last band,
and the stack becomes $\nz(\ell_1+\delta_h,\ell_2,\ldots)$ by
\cref{def:scan-state}(a).  Finally let $x\in I_1$ have local rank $r$.  As noted after \cref{def:scan-state}, $x$ is a $d$-trigger of
$\sigma x$ exceeding $b_d$, so reading it is a split.  No threshold changes,
since $x>b_d$, and the stack becomes $\nz(r-1,\ell_1-r,\ell_2,\ldots)$ by
\cref{def:scan-state}(b).  Every other unread letter lies in a deferred
interval and is illegal by \cref{lem:legal-moves}(b).

(c) By \cref{prop:scan-states}(c), the $\beta_d$-avoiding completions of
$\sigma$ are exactly the sequences of legal moves that read every unread
letter, and by (b) the legal moves from a legal prefix, their successor
states and their multiplicities depend only on $(\mathbf p,L)$ and on the
local ranks of the letters read.  Hence, by induction on the number of
unread letters, which drops by one at every move, the standardized set of
avoiding completions depends only on $(\mathbf p,L)$.
\end{proof}

For $(\mathbf p,L)\ne(\mathbf0,\varnothing)$, partitioning by the next
letter gives
\begin{align}
H_{\mathbf p}(L)={}&
 \sum_{i=0}^{d-2}\sum_{h=0}^{p_i-1}
 H_{T_{i,h}(\mathbf p)}(L)                                           \label{eq:H}\\
&+\sum_{h=0}^{p_{d-1}-1}
 H_{U_h(\mathbf p)}\!\left(
   \nz(\ell_1+\delta_h,\ell_2,\ldots)\right)                       \notag\\
&+\min(2,\ell_1)
 H_{\mathbf p}\!\left(\nz(\ell_1-1,\ell_2,\ldots)\right)       \notag\\
&+\sum_{j=2}^{\ell_1-1}
 H_{\mathbf p}\!\left((j-1,\ell_1-j,\ell_2,\ldots)\right).        \notag
\end{align}
The first double sum is empty when $d=1$, and the last two lines are absent
when the stack is empty.  The four lines represent, respectively, an
early-band update, a last-band trigger and merger, an endpoint choice in the
active interval, and an interior choice.  Empty sums vanish.
The initial and terminal conditions are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:initial-terminal}
 a_n^{(d)}:=|\Av_n(\beta_d)|
 =H_{(n,0,\ldots,0)}(\varnothing),
 \qquad H_{\mathbf0}(\varnothing)=1.
\end{equation}
For $d=1$, identify $H_{(p)}(L)=W_p(L)$.  The early-band sum is empty,
$U_h((p))=(h)$, and \eqref{eq:H}--\eqref{eq:initial-terminal} reduce to
\eqref{eq:W}--\eqref{eq:W-initial}.

\begin{theorem}[Literal recurrence]\label{thm:literal}
For every $d\geq1$, equations \eqref{eq:H} and
\eqref{eq:initial-terminal} count every $\beta_d$-avoiding permutation
exactly once.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{prop:scan-states}(c), the $\beta_d$-avoiding permutations are
exactly the words all of whose prefixes are legal, and by
\cref{prop:state-invariant}(b) the four types of terms in \eqref{eq:H} are
exactly the legal moves.  Local ranks identify letters uniquely,
except that two distinct endpoints have the same standardized successor and
are recorded with multiplicity two.  Interpret this coefficient $2$ as two
labeled transitions, corresponding to local ranks $1$ and $\ell_1$.
Moreover
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rho}
 \rho(\mathbf p,L)=\|\mathbf p\|_1+\sum_{j=1}^{s}\ell_j
\end{equation}
drops by one on every move.  An early-band move reduces the control mass by
one.  A last-band move reduces it by $\delta_h+1$ and adds $\delta_h$ to the
head.  An active-interval move consumes one head value.  Hence every maximal
recurrence path ends at the unique zero state.  Reading the chosen local
ranks along such a path reconstructs one permutation, inverse to scanning
that permutation.
\end{proof}

The stack can be read off the scanned prefix.  Recall that two unread
values are adjacent if no unread value lies strictly between them.  By
\cref{prop:scan-states}(c), the legal prefixes are exactly the prefixes of
$\beta_d$-avoiding permutations, and every legal prefix has an avoiding
completion, so condition (iii) below is not vacuous.

\begin{corollary}[Separations of the stack]\label{cor:separators}
After a legal prefix has been read, let $u<v$ be adjacent unread values
above $b_d$.  The following are equivalent:
(i) $u$ and $v$ lie in different intervals of the stack,
(ii) some letter $x$ with $u<x<v$ was read after a $d$-trigger smaller
than $u$,
(iii) $u$ precedes $v$ in every $\beta_d$-avoiding completion.
In particular, the stack is determined by the scanned prefix: its union is
the set of unread values above $b_d$ by \cref{prop:state-invariant}(a), and
its intervals are separated exactly at the adjacent pairs described in (ii).
Condition (ii) locates the separations, not the letters that produced
them: several letters between $u$ and $v$ may satisfy (ii), and the letter
whose split created the separation need not be among them.  By
\cref{prop:state-invariant}(c), the control $\mathbf p$ together with these
separations determines the completions up to order isomorphism.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Condition (i) implies (iii): by \cref{lem:trigger}, an avoiding completion
satisfies the residual obligation of every $d$-trigger of the prefix, hence
by \cref{prop:state-invariant}(a) lies in the language
\eqref{eq:obligation-language}, which places $I_j$ before $I_k$ for $j<k$.
For (ii) implies (iii), let $c$ be the trigger.  If $v$ preceded $u$ in a
completion, an increasing $d$-subsequence ending at $c$, followed by
$x,v,u$, would form $\beta_d$, since $c<u<x<v$.  For (iii) implies (i),
suppose that $u$ and $v$ lie in the same interval, and complete the prefix
by the avoiding completion constructed in the proof of
\cref{prop:scan-states}(c): the base values in decreasing order, then the
intervals $I_1,I_2,\ldots$ in order, each in decreasing order.  It avoids
$\beta_d$ and reads $v$ before $u$.

For (i) implies (ii), we show along the scan that (i) and (ii) agree for
every adjacent pair above $b_d$.  The induction repeats that of
\cref{lem:1342-separators} with $m$ replaced by $b_d$.  We give it in full
because of the additional early-band case.  Initially the stack is empty
and no trigger has been read.  Let $x$ be the letter read next.  In an early-band
move, which does not occur when $d=1$, $x<b_d$ is not a $d$-trigger and
lies below every unread value above $b_d$, so the stack and both
conditions are unchanged.  In a last-band move, $x$ becomes the least
trigger, and $E$ joins the head or becomes the whole stack when the old
stack was empty.  An adjacent pair $u<v$ above $x$ that was not an adjacent
pair above the old $b_d$ has $u\in E$, so $u$ is smaller than every trigger
read before $x$, and every letter between $u$ and $v$ was read before $x$.
Hence (ii) fails, and (i) fails because $u$ and $v$ lie in the single
interval $E\cup I_1$, or $E$.  All other pairs keep their members, their
intervals, and the letters between them, and $x$ lies below them, so both
conditions are unchanged for them.  In an active-interval move the stack
is nonempty, so $b_d$ is a $d$-trigger by
\cref{lem:least-trigger-frontier}, and $x\in I_1$ is read after it.  Only
the unread neighbours $u<x<v$ of $x$, when both exist and $u>b_d$, can form
a new adjacent pair.  Then $u$ lies in the part of $I_1$ below $x$, while
$v$ lies in the part above $x$ or, if $x=\max I_1$, is the least element
of $I_2$.  Thus $u$ and $v$ lie in different intervals, and $x$ witnesses
(ii) with the trigger $b_d<u$.  All other pairs are unaffected.
\end{proof}

Adjacency is needed only in (ii), as the example after
\cref{lem:1342-separators} shows.  For arbitrary unread $u<v$ above $b_d$,
conditions (i) and (iii) remain equivalent, and they hold exactly when some
pair $a<c$ of adjacent unread values with $u\leq a<c\leq v$ satisfies (ii).

\subsection{Why direct memoization is exponential}\label{sec:fibonacci}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:exponential}
For fixed $d\geq1$ and $n\geq d+1$, literal memoization of \eqref{eq:H} has
at least $F_{n-d}$ reachable composition keys when computing $a_n^{(d)}$,
where $F_{n-d}$ is the $(n-d)$th Fibonacci number, $F_1=F_2=1$.  In
particular, its state space is exponential in $n$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
From $H_{(n,0,\ldots,0)}(\varnothing)$, choose successively the least
unread value in bands $0,1,\ldots,d-1$.  Equivalently, begin with
$1,2,\ldots,d$.  This reaches $H_{\mathbf0}((n-d))$.  The interior moves are
then exactly those used in the proof of \cref{prop:scalar-exponential}, with
$n$ there replaced by $n-d+1$.  They reach every positive composition $L$
satisfying
\[
 |L|+\operatorname{len}(L)=n-d+1,
\]
and their number is
\[
 \sum_{s=1}^{\lfloor(n-d+1)/2\rfloor}
 \binom{n-d-s}{s-1}=F_{n-d}.\qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

\section{Protected tails and transfer kernels}\label{sec:kernels}

For the recurrence \eqref{eq:H}, take $\mathcal C=\N^d$ and let the block
alphabet be the positive integers.  If $L$ is a composition, $(\ell)L$
denotes the result of prepending $\ell$.  Mark the boundary before $L$ and
let $K_\ell^L(\mathbf p,\mathbf t)$ be the number of paths from
$H_{\mathbf p}((\ell)\mid L)$ stopped when $L$ is first exposed, at
$H_{\mathbf t}(\varnothing\mid L)$.  An early-band move changes only the
control.  A last-band move can enlarge the active head, an endpoint can
shorten it, and an interior letter can replace it by two parts.  None of
these transitions changes $L$ before exposure, and \eqref{eq:H} shows that
the transitions leaving a state and their targets depend only on
$\mathbf p$ and the blocks preceding the marker.  Replacing $L$ therefore
gives the bijection of outgoing transitions required by (P2).  Equation \eqref{eq:rho}
proves termination, and the only terminal state has an empty stack.  Hence no
complete path can terminate before exposing $L$.  The hypotheses of
\cref{thm:protected-tail-principle} therefore hold.

\begin{corollary}[Permutation-stack factorization]\label{cor:protected-tail}
The number $K_\ell^L(\mathbf p,\mathbf t)$ is independent of $L$.  Denote it
by $K_\ell(\mathbf p,\mathbf t)$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:factorization}
 H_{\mathbf p}((\ell)L)=
 \sum_{\mathbf t}K_\ell(\mathbf p,\mathbf t)H_{\mathbf t}(L).
\end{equation}
Consequently, for $L=(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_s)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matrix-product}
 H_{\mathbf p}(L)=\sum_{\mathbf t}
 (K_{\ell_1}K_{\ell_2}\cdots K_{\ell_s})(\mathbf p,\mathbf t)
 H_{\mathbf t}(\varnothing).
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

For fixed $\ell$ and $\mathbf p$, the function
$K_\ell(\mathbf p,\mathord\cdot)$ is a \emph{kernel row}.  We call
$\mathbf p$ its \emph{source control}, and an index $\mathbf t$ a
\emph{terminal control}.  Its support is
$\supp K_\ell(\mathbf p,\mathord\cdot)
=\{\mathbf t:K_\ell(\mathbf p,\mathbf t)\ne0\}$.
The factorization replaces the composition argument by a product of transfer
kernels, evaluated on the required source rows.  The next section bounds the
number of source and terminal
controls required for coefficients through degree $N$.

\begin{proposition}[Compressed kernel recurrence]
\label{prop:kernel-recurrence}
For $\ell\geq1$, the transfer kernels satisfy
\begin{align}
K_\ell(\mathbf p,\mathbf t)={}&
 \sum_{i=0}^{d-2}\sum_{h=0}^{p_i-1}
 K_\ell(T_{i,h}(\mathbf p),\mathbf t)                               \label{eq:K}\\
&+\sum_{h=0}^{p_{d-1}-1}
 K_{\ell+\delta_h}(U_h(\mathbf p),\mathbf t)
 +D_\ell(\mathbf p,\mathbf t)                                      \notag\\
&+\sum_{\substack{a,b\geq1\\a+b=\ell-1}}
  \sum_{\mathbf u}K_a(\mathbf p,\mathbf u)
  K_b(\mathbf u,\mathbf t),                                         \notag
\end{align}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:D}
 D_1(\mathbf p,\mathbf t)=\one_{\mathbf p=\mathbf t},
 \qquad
 D_\ell(\mathbf p,\mathbf t)=2K_{\ell-1}(\mathbf p,\mathbf t)
 \quad(\ell\geq2).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Partition a stopped path by its first move.  Early- and last-band moves give
the first two sums in \eqref{eq:K}.  If $\ell=1$, consuming the only head
value exposes the marked tail at the same control.  If $\ell\geq2$, the two
endpoint choices give the same smaller head.  These are the terms in
\eqref{eq:D}.  An interior choice produces two blocks $(a,b)$ with
$a+b=\ell-1$.  Re-mark the resulting state as
$(\mathbf p,(a)\mid(b)L)$.  As in the scalar case, a path stopped at the
first exposure of $L$ passes through a unique first exposure of $(b)L$.
Cutting there and summing over the
intermediate control gives the kernel product in the last line.
\end{proof}

It remains to connect the kernels to an empty stack.  Put
\[
 G_{\mathbf p}=H_{\mathbf p}(\varnothing)
\]
and define the boundary kernel
$K_0(\mathbf p,\mathbf t)=\one_{\mathbf p=\mathbf t}$.

\begin{corollary}[Empty-stack recurrence]\label{cor:empty-stack-recurrence}
The empty-stack values satisfy
\begin{align}
G_{\mathbf p}={}&\one_{\mathbf p=\mathbf0}
 +\sum_{i=0}^{d-2}\sum_{h=0}^{p_i-1}
 G_{T_{i,h}(\mathbf p)}                                               \label{eq:G}\\
&+\sum_{h=0}^{p_{d-1}-1}\sum_{\mathbf t}
 K_{\delta_h}(U_h(\mathbf p),\mathbf t)G_{\mathbf t}.                \notag
\end{align}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply the empty-stack part of \eqref{eq:H}.  When a last-band move creates a
head of size $\delta_h$, use \eqref{eq:factorization}.  The identity kernel
$K_0$ handles $\delta_h=0$.
\end{proof}

The desired answer is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:answer-G}
 a_n^{(d)}=G_{(n,0,\ldots,0)}.
\end{equation}

\section{The family algorithm and its complexity}\label{sec:algorithm}

Again give a kernel source $(\mathbf p,\ell)$ the grade
\[
 w=\|\mathbf p\|_1+\ell.
\]
We verify below that increasing grade is a topological order for the kernel
dependencies in \eqref{eq:K}, and that increasing control mass orders the
empty-stack recurrence \eqref{eq:G}.  The band terms of \eqref{eq:K}
lower the grade by one.  For example,
\[
 \|U_h(\mathbf p)\|_1+(\ell+\delta_h)
 =\|\mathbf p\|_1-(\delta_h+1)+\ell+\delta_h=w-1.
\]
The split term is controlled by the following support bound.

\begin{lemma}[Kernel support]\label{lem:support}
For every $\ell\geq1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:support}
 \supp K_\ell(\mathbf p,\mathord\cdot)
 \subseteq
 \{\mathbf p\}\cup
 \{\mathbf t:\|\mathbf t\|_1\leq\|\mathbf p\|_1-1\}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Every base move consumes a letter and hence strictly lowers control mass.
Active-interval moves leave the control unchanged.  If no base move occurs,
the terminal control is $\mathbf p$.  If at least one base move occurs, the
terminal control has mass at most $\|\mathbf p\|_1-1$.
\end{proof}

The dynamic program uses the control coordinates above.  Each row operation
below is coefficientwise, and every uninitialized row is zero.

\paragraph{Kernel phase.}
For $w=1,2,\ldots,N$, and every $\mathbf p\in\N^d$, $\ell\geq1$ with
$\|\mathbf p\|_1+\ell=w$, evaluate \eqref{eq:K} coefficientwise and store
the resulting sparse row.  In each split term, for every $a,b\geq1$ with
$a+b=\ell-1$ and
$\mathbf u\in\supp K_a(\mathbf p,\mathord\cdot)$, add the scalar multiple
$K_a(\mathbf p,\mathbf u)K_b(\mathbf u,\mathord\cdot)$ to the row being
computed.

\paragraph{Empty-stack phase.}
Set $G_{\mathbf0}=1$.  For $m=1,2,\ldots,N$, and every
$\mathbf p\in\N^d$ with $\|\mathbf p\|_1=m$, evaluate \eqref{eq:G}, using
$K_0$ as the identity kernel.  Return
$(G_{(n,0,\ldots,0)})_{0\leq n\leq N}$.

In the kernel phase, the early- and last-band rows in \eqref{eq:K} have
grade $w-1$.  The term $D_1$ is initial data, while $D_\ell$ for
$\ell\geq2$ uses a row of grade $w-1$.  In a split term,
$\|\mathbf p\|_1+a<w$ and, by \cref{lem:support},
$\|\mathbf u\|_1+b\leq\|\mathbf p\|_1+b<w$.  Hence increasing $w$ is a
topological order.  In the empty-stack phase every terminal control used at
mass $m$ has smaller mass, including $U_h(\mathbf p)$ when $K_0$ occurs.
Thus
increasing $m$ is topological.

For coefficients through size $N$, we use the padded region
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source-region}
 \mathbf p\in\N^d,\qquad \ell\geq1,\qquad
 \|\mathbf p\|_1+\ell\leq N.
\end{equation}
The region contains exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:row-count}
 \binom{N+d}{d+1}=O_d(N^{d+1})
\end{equation}
rows, by stars and bars.  By \cref{lem:support}, a row has at most
$1+\binom{\|\mathbf p\|_1+d-1}{d}=O_d(N^d)$ possible terminal controls.

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:main}]
Correctness follows from \cref{thm:literal,cor:protected-tail}: equations
\eqref{eq:K} and \eqref{eq:G} are the first-step evaluations of the exact
factorization \eqref{eq:factorization}, and the preceding topological orders
evaluate all of their dependencies.  It remains to bound the resources.

There are $O_d(N^{d+1})$ source rows and $O_d(N^d)$ possible entries per
row, giving $O_d(N^{2d+1})$ stored integers.  In a split convolution there
are $O(N)$ choices of $a$, $O_d(N^d)$ intermediate controls $\mathbf u$,
and $O_d(N^d)$ entries in the row added for each $\mathbf u$.  Thus a direct
sparse implementation uses $O_d(N^{2d+1})$ operations per source row and
$O_d(N^{3d+2})$ over all kernels.  The nonsplit part uses $O_d(N)$ row
additions per source and therefore $O_d(N^{2d+2})$ operations in total.  For
each of the $O_d(N^d)$ empty-stack controls,
\eqref{eq:G} sums at most $O(N)$ choices over $O_d(N^d)$ terminal controls,
so the $G$ table costs
$O_d(N^{2d+1})$.  For $d\geq1$, the exponents $2d+2$ and $2d+1$ are both
smaller than $3d+2$.

For a complete path in \eqref{eq:H}, define its workload by \eqref{eq:rho}.  For a
stopped kernel path, define its workload to be the control mass plus the sum
of the sizes of the blocks preceding the marked suffix.  Each initial
workload is at most $N$ and decreases by one at every transition.  At a
state of \eqref{eq:H}, the number of outgoing transitions is the number of
legal next letters and is at most the workload.  Hence a path has at
most $N$ edges and at most $N$ labeled choices at each nonterminal state.
Each stored $G_{\mathbf p}$ counts complete paths in \eqref{eq:H}, while each
stored kernel entry counts stopped kernel paths.  Every such integer is at most
$(N+1)^N$ and has
$O(N\log N)$ bits.  Every intermediate product and partial sum is a
nonnegative contribution to a stored value and obeys the same bound.
Replacing integer multiplication by its
$M(N\log N)$ bit cost proves the stated bit time.  The bit storage is
$O_d(N^{2d+2}\log N)$.
\end{proof}

\section{The two-threshold quotient for
\texorpdfstring{$12453$}{12453}}\label{sec:d2-support}

For $d=2$, write $\mathbf p=(p,q)$.  Two exact symmetries of the kernels are
available in this case, and they serve different purposes.  The first is a
translation in the lower threshold coordinate: a kernel entry depends on the
first coordinates of its source and terminal controls only through their
difference, so one coordinate can be dropped from the table.  This is the
symmetry that improves the direct $d=2$ specialization $O(N^8)$ and $O(N^5)$
of \cref{thm:main} to the $O(N^7)$ and $O(N^4)$ of \cref{cor:d2-complexity}.
The second is a partial translation in the upper coordinate.  It leaves
those exponents unchanged and halves the leading constant in the storage count.
We first determine the exact support of a kernel row: the bound of
\cref{lem:support} has the following exact refinement.

\begin{lemma}[Exact two-threshold support]\label{lem:d2-exact-support}
For every $\ell\geq1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:support-d2}
 \supp K_\ell((p,q),\mathord\cdot)
 =\{(p,q)\}\cup
 \{(u,v):0\leq u\leq p,\ v\geq0,\ u+v\leq p+q-1\}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A base move never increases the first coordinate and strictly lowers
the coordinate sum, proving necessity.  The terminal control $(p,q)$ is
reached by using no base move and selecting active-interval endpoints until
the interval is empty.  To reach a control $(u,v)$ in the second set with $u<p$, first
update the minimum so that $u$ unread values remain below it.
This gives $(u,Q)$ with $Q=p+q-1-u$.  The support inequality says $v\leq Q$.
If $v=Q$, make no further base move.  If $v<Q$, make the last-band move with
local parameter $h=v$.  If $u=p$, then $v\leq q-1$, so make that last-band
move directly.  Finally, exhaust each active interval by repeated endpoint
choices.  This reaches the marked suffix at every stated terminal
control.
\end{proof}

We now prove the first translation.

\begin{lemma}[First-coordinate translation]\label{lem:d2-translation}
For $\ell\geq1$ and $0\leq c\leq p$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:d2-translation}
 K_\ell((p,q),(c,s))=K_\ell((p-c,q),(0,s)).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Along a kernel path ending at $(c,s)$, the first control coordinate never
falls below $c$.  Delete the $c$ lowest unread values throughout the path and
shift all remaining labels down by $c$.  This preserves the active interval
sizes, every transition multiplicity, and the second control coordinate, and
gives a path from $(p-c,q)$ to $(0,s)$.  The inverse operation adjoins $c$
unread values below every value used by the path.  These added values are
never selected, because the terminal first coordinate is $c$.  They shift by
exactly $c$ the parameter $h$ of every band-$0$ letter, leave the parameter
$r$ of every last-band letter and the local ranks within the active head
unchanged, and preserve every transition multiplicity.  The
two operations are inverse.
\end{proof}

Write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:R-definition}
 R_{\ell,a}(q,s):=K_\ell((a,q),(0,s)).
\end{equation}
We call $R_{\ell,a}(q,s)$ a \emph{reduced entry} and the table of all such
entries the \emph{first-coordinate quotient}.  From here on the letter $a$
denotes a reduced first control coordinate, not a block size as in
\eqref{eq:W} and \eqref{eq:K}.  Block sizes in a split are written
$\ell_1,\ell_2$.  By \cref{lem:d2-translation}, every kernel entry is
represented by one such reduced entry, with $a=p-c$.  For a reduced source
$(\ell,a,q)$ with $a=0$,
the possible terminal coordinates are
$0\leq s\leq q$.  If $a>0$, a base move is necessary and
$0\leq s\leq a+q-1$.  The first-coordinate quotient therefore has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:d2-first-reduced-counts}
 \binom{N+2}{3}\quad\hbox{rows},
 \qquad
 \frac{N(N+1)(N^2+N+4)}{12}\quad\hbox{entries}.
\end{equation}
The entry formula follows by summing $q+1$ for $a=0$ and $a+q$ for $a>0$
over the same padded source region.  In particular, it already gives
$O(N^4)$ storage.  The operation count will be recorded after the second
translation.

We first rewrite the kernel recurrence in the reduced variables.  In the notation
of \eqref{eq:R-definition}, apply \cref{lem:d2-translation} to
\eqref{eq:K}.  An early-band move from $(a,q)$ gives
$R_{\ell,h}(a+q-h-1,s)$, and a last-band move gives
$R_{\ell+q-r-1,a}(r,s)$.  In a split with intermediate control $(c,m)$,
the first factor $K_{\ell_1}((a,q),(c,m))$ vanishes unless $c\leq a$, since
both parts of the support \eqref{eq:support-d2} have first coordinate at
most $a$.  For $0\leq c\leq a$
the two factors become
\[
 R_{\ell_1,a-c}(q,m)R_{\ell_2,c}(m,s).
\]
Writing $a_1=a-c$ and $a_2=c$ therefore gives the reduced recurrence
\begin{align}
R_{\ell,a}(q,s)={}&
 \sum_{h=0}^{a-1}R_{\ell,h}(a+q-h-1,s)
 +\sum_{r=0}^{q-1}R_{\ell+q-r-1,a}(r,s)
 +D_{\ell,a}(q,s)                                      \label{eq:R-recurrence}\\
&+\sum_{\substack{\ell_1,\ell_2\geq1\\
                    \ell_1+\ell_2=\ell-1}}
  \ \sum_{\substack{a_1,a_2\geq0\\a_1+a_2=a}}\ \sum_{m\geq0}
  R_{\ell_1,a_1}(q,m)R_{\ell_2,a_2}(m,s),             \notag
\end{align}
where
\[
 D_{1,a}(q,s)=\one_{a=0,\,q=s},
 \qquad
 D_{\ell,a}(q,s)=2R_{\ell-1,a}(q,s)\quad(\ell\geq2),
\]
and entries outside the support above are zero.

The reduced grade $\ell+a+q$ is a topological order for
\eqref{eq:R-recurrence}: every term of the first two sums and every $D$ term
has reduced grade $\ell+a+q-1$, while in a split the first factor has grade
$\ell_1+a_1+q\leq\ell+a+q-2$ and, since a nonzero first factor forces
$m\leq q$ when $a_1=0$ and $m\leq a_1+q-1$ when $a_1>0$, the second factor
has grade $\ell_2+a_2+m\leq\ell+a+q-2$.

The second control coordinate admits a partial quotient as well: raising
both the second source coordinate and the terminal coordinate by one leaves
a reduced entry unchanged whenever the terminal coordinate is at least
$a-1$.

\begin{lemma}[Second-coordinate translation]\label{lem:d2-second-translation}
For $\ell\geq1$ and $a,q,s\geq0$, whenever $s\geq a-1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:d2-second-translation}
 R_{\ell,a}(q+1,s+1)=R_{\ell,a}(q,s).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $\gamma=\ell+a+q+1$, the reduced grade of the left-hand side, and induct on
$\gamma\geq2$.  If $\gamma=2$, then $(\ell,a,q)=(1,0,0)$, and
\eqref{eq:R-recurrence} gives
\[
 R_{1,0}(1,s+1)=\one_{s=0}=R_{1,0}(0,s).
\]
Assume henceforth that $\gamma>2$ and that the claim holds at every smaller
reduced grade.  The $h$th term of the first sum on the left is
$R_{\ell,h}(a+q-h,s+1)$.  Since $s\geq a-1\geq h-1$, induction translates
this to $R_{\ell,h}(a+q-h-1,s)$, the corresponding term on the right.

In the second sum, the $r=0$ term on the left vanishes: at second source
coordinate zero the largest possible terminal coordinate is $0$ if $a=0$,
and $a-1$
otherwise, whereas the terminal coordinate satisfies $s+1\geq1$ and
$s+1\geq a$.  Put $r=j+1$ in the remaining terms.
Induction gives
\[
 R_{\ell+q-j-1,a}(j+1,s+1)
 =R_{\ell+q-j-1,a}(j,s),
\]
which gives exactly the terms $0\leq j<q$ on the right.  The $D$ terms also
match: for $\ell=1$ both are $\one_{a=0,\,q=s}$, while for $\ell>1$ the
claim follows from induction applied to $2R_{\ell-1,a}$.

It remains to compare one split with $a=a_1+a_2$.  On the left it contributes
\[
 \sum_{m\geq0}R_{\ell_1,a_1}(q+1,m)
                  R_{\ell_2,a_2}(m,s+1).
\]
The $m=0$ term vanishes by the support of the second factor.  Put $m=k+1$.
If that second factor is nonzero, then $k\geq a_1-1$.  Indeed, when $a_2=0$,
support gives $k\geq s\geq a_1-1$, while for $a_2>0$ it gives
$k\geq s+1-a_2\geq a_1$.  Also $s\geq a-1\geq a_2-1$.  Thus the induction
hypothesis applies to both factors.

Both induction calls are at smaller reduced grade.  The first has grade
$\ell_1+a_1+q+1<\gamma$.  Nonvanishing of that factor also gives
\[
 k\leq q\quad\text{if }a_1=0,
 \qquad
 k\leq a_1+q-1\quad\text{if }a_1>0,
\]
by \eqref{eq:support-d2}.  Hence the second factor has reduced grade
$\ell_2+a_2+k+1<\gamma$.  Induction therefore gives
\[
 R_{\ell_1,a_1}(q+1,k+1)R_{\ell_2,a_2}(k+1,s+1)
 =R_{\ell_1,a_1}(q,k)R_{\ell_2,a_2}(k,s).
\]
The same upper and lower support bounds hold for every nonzero term on the
right, so this is a bijective reindexing of the split sum.  The induction is
complete.
\end{proof}

The hypothesis $s\geq a-1$ cannot be weakened: equation
\eqref{eq:R-recurrence} gives $R_{1,2}(1,1)=3$ but $R_{1,2}(0,0)=2$.

For $a=0$ and $0\leq s\leq q$, \cref{lem:d2-second-translation} gives
$R_{\ell,0}(q,s)=R_{\ell,0}(q-s,0)$.  For $a\geq1$ and $s\geq a$, it gives
\[
 R_{\ell,a}(q,s)=R_{\ell,a}(q-s+a-1,a-1),
\]
where the support condition ensures that the new second source coordinate is
nonnegative.  Thus we retain only terminal coordinate $s=0$ when $a=0$, and
terminal coordinates
$0\leq s<a$ when $a\geq1$.  The exact number of retained entries is
\begin{align}
 \binom{N+1}{2}+\binom{N+2}{4}
 &=\frac{N(N+1)(N^2+N+10)}{24}.             \label{eq:d2-reduced-counts}
\end{align}
Thus the second translation halves the leading storage constant relative to
the first-coordinate quotient.  Exact polynomials for loop iterations and
stored entries are recorded in the computation report in the supplement.

\begin{corollary}[Sharpened $12453$ bound]\label{cor:d2-complexity}
The numbers $|\Av_n(12453)|$ for $0\leq n\leq N$ can be computed using
$O(N^7)$ exact-integer arithmetic operations and $O(N^4)$ stored integers.
The bit time is $O(N^7M(N\log N))$, and the bit storage is
$O(N^5\log N)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Use the first-coordinate quotient in this operation count, evaluating
\eqref{eq:R-recurrence} in increasing reduced grade, which the preceding
paragraphs showed to be a topological order.  It has
$O(N^3)$ source rows and $O(N^4)$ stored entries by
\eqref{eq:d2-first-reduced-counts}.  A direct split evaluation has $O(N)$ choices for
the block split $\ell_1$, $O(N)$ choices for the control split $a_1$, and
$O(N)$ possible intermediate second coordinates $m$.  For each such triple,
adding the row $R_{\ell_2,a_2}(m,\mathord\cdot)$ uses $O(N)$ possible terminal
coordinates.  Thus one source row costs $O(N^4)$ operations and all split
terms cost $O(N^7)$.  The second-coordinate translation can reduce the
storage constant.  Each omitted entry is recovered from
\eqref{eq:d2-second-translation} in $O(1)$ operations.  Direct evaluation of
the nonsplit kernel terms costs at most $O(N^5)$.  The empty-stack table has
$O(N^2)$ source controls, $O(N)$ last-band choices, and $O(N^2)$ possible
terminal controls, so it also costs $O(N^5)$.  The path-size argument
in the proof of \cref{thm:main} gives $O(N\log N)$ bits per integer, yielding
the asserted bit bounds.
\end{proof}

\section{Certified computation for \texorpdfstring{$\Av(12453)$}{Av(12453)}}
\label{sec:computations}

The reduced recurrence was evaluated modulo pairwise distinct primes and then
reconstructed over the integers.  The programs
\path{av12453_fast_rns.cpp} and \path{reconstruct_av12453_rns.py} in the
supplement perform the
modular recurrence evaluation, verification of the coefficient bound, and
reconstruction.  Separately written implementations provide
error-detection checks of the multi-prime program.  They are listed at the
end of this section.
Complete source, execution logs,
residues, and machine-readable output are included in the supplement.

\begin{proposition}[Computer-assisted certification]
\label{prop:exact-150}
The file \path{code/data/av12453_terms_0_150.txt} in the supplement contains
the exact values
$|\Av_n(12453)|$ for every $0\leq n\leq150$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put $b_m=|\Av_m(1342)|$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-crt-bound}
 B_N:=\sum_{m=0}^N\binom Nm^2 b_m.
\end{equation}
Deleting the left-to-right minima of a $12453$-avoider leaves a
$1342$-avoider \cite{Bona2005}: a $1342$ among the remaining entries could
be preceded by an earlier smaller entry, producing a $12453$.  If $m$
entries remain, choose
their positions, their values, and their standardization.  The deleted
minima occupy the remaining positions, and their remaining values are forced
in decreasing order.  Hence $|\Av_N(12453)|\leq B_N$.

The integers $b_m$ are generated exactly from \eqref{eq:bona-1342}.  The
source \path{av12453_fast_rns.cpp} implements
\eqref{eq:R-recurrence} and the empty-stack recurrence \eqref{eq:G} modulo
each selected prime.  By
\cref{thm:literal,cor:protected-tail,prop:kernel-recurrence,%
cor:empty-stack-recurrence,lem:d2-translation,lem:d2-second-translation},
its outputs are the residues of the desired coefficients.  The moduli are
the twenty-four largest primes below $2^{31}$, recorded with the residue
packs in the supplement.  The program
\path{reconstruct_av12453_rns.py} verifies that the product $\mathcal M$ of
the eighteen largest exceeds $B_{150}$.  In fact
$\mathcal M/B_{150}>1.92$.  Since the nonnegative bound $B_N$ is
nondecreasing, every coefficient through degree $150$ lies in
$[0,\mathcal M)$.  The Chinese remainder theorem therefore determines each
coefficient uniquely.

The bound and the Chinese remainder theorem therefore give the unique integer
lifts.  The six remaining primes, withheld from reconstruction, agree
with every reconstructed coefficient.  The separately written single-modulus
$d=2$ program computes all $151$ coefficients through degree $150$ modulo
$2^{61}-1$ and also agrees.
These are additional error-detection checks, not inputs to the uniqueness
argument.
\end{proof}

Brute-force enumeration and \eqref{eq:H} give the same counts through $n=8$
for $d=1,2,3,4$.  Separately written Python and C++ implementations of the
unreduced family recurrence agree with one another through $n=10$.  For
$d=2$, the coefficients through $n=38$ agree with OEIS A116485 and
Biers-Ariel's composition recurrence
\cite{BiersAriel2019,BiersArielA116485,OEISA116485}.  The first-coordinate quotient and the
unreduced family kernel give identical $d=2$ coefficients through $n=40$.
The dense modular program, which uses neither the second translation nor the
storage quotient from \cref{lem:d2-second-translation}, and the
single-modulus program give identical residues modulo $2^{61}-1$ through
$n=60$.

\subsection{Uniform random generation}\label{sec:sampling}

The transfer tables also generate uniformly random avoiders.  By
\cref{thm:literal}, the labeled complete paths of \eqref{eq:H} from the
initial state $H_{(n,0,\ldots,0)}(\varnothing)$, with the two endpoint
choices counted as distinct transitions as in its proof, correspond
bijectively to the $\beta_d$-avoiding permutations of length $n$.  By the
recursive method \cite{NijenhuisWilf1978}, such a path is uniformly random
when every transition is chosen with probability proportional to the number
of complete paths through it, that is, to the value of the
corresponding summand of \eqref{eq:H}.  That value is a completion count
$H_{\mathbf p}(L)$ for a stack $L$ of arbitrary length, which the
factorization \eqref{eq:matrix-product} expresses through the stored
kernels.  Equivalently, one samples a complete path of the factorized
recurrence itself.  The first-move partitions in the proofs of
\cref{prop:kernel-recurrence,cor:empty-stack-recurrence} identify the
summands of \eqref{eq:K} and \eqref{eq:G}, with the coefficient-$2$
endpoint term counted as two summands, with a partition of the stopped and
complete paths, and iterating the cut-and-concatenate bijection of
\cref{thm:protected-tail-principle} maps these paths onto the complete
paths of \eqref{eq:H}.  At an empty-stack state, a summand of \eqref{eq:G}
is chosen with probability proportional to its value.  This selects a base
letter and, for a last-band move, the terminal control $\mathbf t$ at which
the new head will have been exhausted.  Inside a kernel
$K_\ell(\mathbf p,\mathbf t)$, a summand of \eqref{eq:K} is chosen in the
same way.  This selects a base move, an endpoint of the active head, or an
interior letter of it together with the intermediate control $\mathbf u$ of
the split, and each pending block carries the control at which it must be
exposed.  By induction on the grade, a given stopped path of
$K_\ell(\mathbf p,\mathbf t)$ is produced with probability
$1/K_\ell(\mathbf p,\mathbf t)$ and a given complete path from
$G_{\mathbf p}$ with probability $1/G_{\mathbf p}$.  At
$\mathbf p=(n,0,\ldots,0)$ this is $1/|\Av_n(\beta_d)|$.

\begin{proposition}[Uniform random generation]\label{prop:sampling}
Let $N\geq n\geq0$.  Once the first-coordinate quotient $R_{\ell,a}(q,s)$ of
\cref{sec:d2-support} and the empty-stack values $G_{(p,q)}$ have been
computed for size $N$, a uniformly random element of $\Av_n(12453)$ can be
produced with $O(n^4)$ arithmetic operations on the stored integers.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Uniformity is the preceding paragraph.  A permutation of length $n$ is
produced by $n$ letter choices.  Each choice scans the summands of one
instance of \eqref{eq:G} or \eqref{eq:K} in a fixed order, the endpoint
term counted as two summands, and stops at the first summand whose running
total exceeds a uniformly random integer $U$ with $0\leq U<T$, where $T$ is
the stored value of the state.  Every summand is one stored entry, twice
one, or a product of two, by \cref{lem:d2-translation}.  Every state
reached from $(n,0)$ has grade at most $n$.  At an empty-stack state the
last-band terms range over the band position and the terminal control,
$O(n^3)$ summands.  Inside a kernel the interior terms range over the split
position and the intermediate control, again $O(n^3)$.  The other groups are
smaller.  Hence each choice costs $O(n^3)$ operations and a sample costs
$O(n^4)$.
\end{proof}


Unlike the coefficients of \cref{prop:exact-150}, our sampler runs in
floating point.  \Cref{prop:sampling} is the exact-arithmetic statement.  The
implementation evaluates the two tables in binary64 with an exact
power-of-two scaling per grade, so that the entries up to $N=300$ stay
within range.  Every summand of the reduced recurrence is nonnegative, so no
cancellation occurs, and the floating-point values $G_{(n,0)}$ agree with
the certified coefficients of \cref{prop:exact-150} through $n=150$ to about
$10^{-11}$ relative error.  These comparisons are an empirical accuracy
check.  We have not established a rigorous bound on the distance of the
sampling distribution from uniformity.  Every sample is verified to
avoid $12453$ by an independent test based on \cref{lem:trigger}, and the
sampler was checked against exhaustive enumeration for small $n$.  Building
the tables for $N=300$ took about four hours on eight cores.  One million
samples of length $300$ then took about thirty-five minutes.
\Cref{fig:heatmap} shows the resulting position--value heatmap, rendered

\section{Formal verification}\label{sec:formal}

The counting recurrences of this paper for $d\leq2$ have been verified in
the Lean~4 proof assistant \cite{Lean4} with the Mathlib library
\cite{Mathlib}.  The development, in the directory \path{formal/} of the
supplement, works with words over $\mathbb N$ and $0$-based values.  It
defines pattern containment as the existence of an order-isomorphic
subsequence, a finite set of $0$-based words in bijection with $\Av_n(\tau)$,
the one-threshold recurrence $W$ of
\eqref{eq:W}, the literal recurrence $H$ of \eqref{eq:H} for $d=2$, and the
kernel recurrences \eqref{eq:scalar-K}--\eqref{eq:scalar-G} for $d=1$ and
\eqref{eq:K}--\eqref{eq:G} for $d=2$.  In the two kernel systems every sum
over controls is restricted to the controls of mass at most $\|\mathbf p\|_1$
(to $\{0,\ldots,p\}$ for $d=1$), which
\cref{lem:scalar-support,lem:support} show loses nothing.
Its four main theorems are, for every $n$,
\begin{align*}
 W_n(\varnothing)&=|\Av_n(1342)|,
 &G_n&=|\Av_n(1342)|,\\
 H_{(n,0)}(\varnothing)&=|\Av_n(12453)|,
 &G_{(n,0)}&=|\Av_n(12453)|,
\end{align*}
that is, \cref{thm:literal} for $d\leq2$ and the correctness of the
recurrences evaluated by the algorithm of \cref{sec:algorithm}
(\eqref{eq:scalar-G} and \eqref{eq:answer-G}) for $1342$ and $12453$.
Their Lean names are \texttt{av1342\_count}, \texttt{av1342\_count\_kernel},
\texttt{av12453\_count}, and \texttt{av12453\_count\_kernel}.

The Lean project \path{formal/Av12453/} consists of twenty modules in three
groups.  The modules \path{Basic.lean}, \path{Trigger.lean}, and
\path{FirstLetter.lean} define words, order isomorphism, containment, the
avoiders, and the patterns $\beta_d$, and prove
\cref{lem:trigger,lem:first-letter}.  The eight modules of
\path{OneThreshold/} treat $d=1$.  The module \path{Defs.lean} defines the
scan, its state $(p,L)$, and $W$.  The modules \path{Invariant.lean},
\path{Semantics.lean}, and \path{Counting.lean} prove the separation
invariant of
\cref{lem:1342-separators}, parts (b) and (c) of \cref{prop:scan-states}, and
\texttt{av1342\_count}.  The modules \path{Kernel.lean},
\path{KernelSupport.lean}, \path{KernelFactor.lean}, and
\path{KernelCount.lean} define $K$, $D$, and
$G$, and prove \cref{lem:scalar-support}, the factorization
\eqref{eq:scalar-factorization}, and \texttt{av1342\_count\_kernel}.  The
nine modules of \path{TwoThreshold/} repeat this for $d=2$, with
\path{Thresholds.lean} for the two thresholds and their bands
(\cref{sec:frontier}) and \path{Defs.lean} for $H$.  The directory
also contains \path{Axioms.lean}, which walks every constant declared in the
twenty modules and fails if any depends on \texttt{sorryAx} or on an axiom
other than propositional extensionality, choice, and quotient soundness.
The script \path{check.sh} builds the project, requires the build to
be free of warnings, runs the axiom sweep, and rejects any occurrence of
\texttt{sorry} or \texttt{native\_decide} in the sources.  There is also a
\path{README.md} with a table of the modules and their headline theorems, and
an independent audit report.  The Lean toolchain (version 4.33.1) and the Mathlib revision
are pinned in the project files.

Two things are left to a human reader.  The first is to run \path{check.sh},
or equivalently \texttt{lake build} followed by the axiom sweep, and see it
end with the line \texttt{CERTIFICATE CHECK PASSED}.  The second is to read,
against this paper, the definitions that occur in the four theorem
statements.  Nothing else needs to be read to know what the theorems say.
They are listed by file.  The module \path{Basic.lean} defines
\texttt{OrderIso}, \texttt{Contains}, \texttt{Avoids}, \texttt{IsPermOf},
\texttt{iota}, and \texttt{beta}, so that \texttt{beta 2} is the $0$-based
word $0,1,3,4,2$ of $12453$.  The module \path{OneThreshold/Defs.lean}
defines \texttt{perms}, \texttt{avoiders}, \texttt{nz}, and \texttt{W}, and
\path{TwoThreshold/Defs.lean} defines \texttt{H}.  The module
\path{OneThreshold/Kernel.lean} defines \texttt{K}, \texttt{D}, and
\texttt{G} for $d=1$, and \path{TwoThreshold/Kernel.lean} defines the control
sets \texttt{ctrls} and \texttt{S} and \texttt{K}, \texttt{D}, and \texttt{G}
for $d=2$.  These definitions occupy about seventy lines of Lean in all.  The
recurrences are written with an explicit recursion bound: \texttt{W},
\texttt{H}, \texttt{K}, and \texttt{G} evaluate an auxiliary function at a
bound exceeding the grade of the argument, and the development proves that
every sufficient bound gives the same value, so the reader need only compare
the equations of the auxiliary functions with the displays \eqref{eq:W},
\eqref{eq:H}, \eqref{eq:scalar-K}--\eqref{eq:scalar-G}, and
\eqref{eq:K}--\eqref{eq:G}.  Each definition carries a docstring giving
this dictionary, with the $0$-based conventions spelled out, and the
displays are also restated as proved theorems
(\texttt{K\_succ\_eq}, \texttt{G\_eq}, and their analogues), which serve as
reading aids but are not part of what must be trusted.

The proofs follow the paper.  The state of a scanned prefix is defined from
the prefix: the thresholds $b_1,b_2$ as the least final values of increasing
subsequences, the bands, and the interval stack by the legal moves of
\cref{def:scan-state}, justified by \cref{lem:first-letter,lem:merger}.  The inductive invariant is the
separation criterion of \cref{lem:1342-separators,cor:separators}, from
which two facts are derived, \cref{prop:scan-states}(b) and (c): a letter
from a deferred interval leaves no avoiding completion, and every complete
word produced by legal moves avoids $\beta_d$.  The completion counts then satisfy the literal recurrence by
induction on the number of unread values, and the factorization
\eqref{eq:factorization} is proved directly from the equations of $H$ by
induction on the grade, without the abstract
\cref{thm:protected-tail-principle}.  The kernels are defined by their
recurrences, so the theorems concern the recurrences as displayed.  The
restriction of the control sums is proved lossless, and it is also what
makes the grade a well-founded order for \eqref{eq:K} read as a definition.

The development has over ten thousand lines in twenty modules, contains no
unproved statement, and each theorem depends only on Lean's three standard
axioms, as reported by the axiom sweep.  The definitions were also
exercised by kernel evaluation: the scans of the running example
\eqref{eq:running-example} reproduce the rows of
\cref{ex:1342-scan,ex:full-state}, kernel evaluation of the recurrences
reproduces the first seven terms of \eqref{eq:first-terms}, and compiled
evaluation the first ten.  Not formalized are the complexity statements of
\cref{thm:main,cor:d2-complexity}, the translation quotients of
\cref{sec:d2-support} and hence the program of \cref{prop:exact-150},
\cref{thm:protected-tail-principle} in its general form, and the family for
$d\geq3$.

\section{Consequences and outlook}\label{sec:discussion}

Two patterns are \emph{Wilf-equivalent} if they have equally many avoiders
of every length.  Reverse, complement, and inverse preserve all counting
sequences.  The dihedral orbit of $12453$ has eight elements, and the
prefix-reversal equivalence of Backelin--West--Xin
\cite{BackelinWestXin2007} gives
$12453=12 \oplus 231 \sim 21 \oplus 231=21453$, whose orbit supplies eight more.  Thus the
algorithm, bounds, and certified coefficients apply to all sixteen members
of the length-five Wilf class represented by $12453$.

B\'ona determined the Stanley--Wilf limit of every member of the
family \cite{Bona2005}:
\begin{equation*}
 \lim_{n\to\infty}\bigl(a_n^{(d)}\bigr)^{1/n}=(d-1+\sqrt{8})^2 .
\end{equation*}
For the remainder of this section, put
$a_n=a_n^{(2)}=|\Av_n(12453)|$ and $\mu=(1+\sqrt{8})^2=9+4\sqrt{2}$.
Guided by the 2022
series analysis
\cite{ClisbyConwayGuttmannInoue2022}, the coefficients through $n=100$
suggest the following refinement.

\begin{conjecture}\label{conj:12453-asymptotic}
There are constants $C,\kappa>0$ and $h\in\mathbb R$ such that, as
$n\to\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:12453-asymptotic}
 a_n=C\mu^n e^{-\kappa n^{1/3}}n^{-17/4}
 \left(1+h n^{-1/3}+O(n^{-2/3})\right).
\end{equation}
\end{conjecture}

The exponents $1/3$ and $-17/4$ were selected using only
$a_0,\ldots,a_{100}$.  The parameters $\kappa,\log C,h$ were obtained by
unweighted least squares on $70\leq n\leq100$ for the model
$\log a_n=n\log\mu-\kappa n^{1/3}-\tfrac{17}{4}\log n+\log C+h n^{-1/3}$,
and were fixed before $a_{101},\ldots,a_{150}$ were computed.  The frozen
values are $(\kappa,C,h)=(1.34550865,\ 0.82710184,\ 1.36800770)$ to the
digits shown.
On those fifty excluded coefficients, the maximum absolute difference
between the observed and predicted values of $\log a_n$ is below
$4.3\cdot10^{-7}$.  See the supplement.  From shorter series, the 2022
analysis estimated the stretched-exponential base $e^{-\kappa}$ (their $\mu_1$)
between $0.26$ and $0.29$ and the power-law exponent as $-4.67\pm0.1$
\cite{ClisbyConwayGuttmannInoue2022}.  The frozen fit gives
$e^{-\kappa}\approx0.260$, at the lower end of that range.  Its exponent
$-17/4$ differs from the 2022 estimate, but the two should be regarded as
consistent rather than in conflict.  On $70\leq n\leq150$ the exponent
$-17/4$ fits the model with one correction term far better than $-4.67$
does, but the preference shrinks by more than an order of magnitude once a
second correction term $n^{-2/3}$ is admitted, and in fits with two
correction terms a change in the stretched exponent $1/3$ is largely
compensated by changes in the power-law exponent and in $\kappa$.  The
inferred power-law exponent is thus sensitive to the correction terms
included, and the available coefficients do not determine it reliably.  We record $-17/4$ as the simplest value consistent with the exact
coefficients, not as a sharpening of the 2022 estimate.  This supports
\cref{conj:12453-asymptotic} but proves neither it nor any D-finiteness claim.

We close with four open problems.  The first is to prove
\cref{conj:12453-asymptotic}.  The second is to lower the arithmetic
exponent $7$ in the $12453$ time bound.  The third is to extend the
certified series beyond $n=150$.  The fourth is to identify other
length-five classes with protected tails, for instance among
the classes generated by container machines
\cite{AlbertHombergerPantoneSharVatter2018}, which by Theorem~1.1 of that paper are
exactly the classes whose basis elements each begin with their largest entry.
